## CPO Reading Notes (what to extract from this document)

The agent should treat the transcript above as the *promised value* and mine it for:

- **The promise (perceived value):** "Turn strangers into high-paying clients 24/7," "without lifting a finger," "you only pay after you see it work," "guaranteed 1,000 conversations in 12 months." Every one of these is a promise the product/delivery must close the gap on.
- **The offer architecture:** entry product ($10–$100) → mid-ticket AI subscription ($199–$499/mo) → high-ticket personal offer. 3-step delivery (Build the Brain → Build the ecosystem → Drive leads), launch in <30 days, Brain built in ~2 weeks.
- **The guarantee & risk reversal:** pay-after-you-see-it (90–100% built before full payment); free-until-1,000-conversations; benchmark ascension 10–20% mid-ticket, 5–10% high-ticket (explicitly *not* guaranteed).
- **The two sides of the product, in the company's own words:** the *expert buyer* ("stay in full control," "approve voice/style," "~1 hr/week," "nothing it says is something you wouldn't say") vs. the *consumer lead* (the 90-second diagnostic, the £17 buy, the 24–48hr nurture, the ascension).
- **The dominant emotions sold on:** freedom from being the bottleneck, fear of being left behind ("first to market"), status/credibility (named alongside Hyman/Robbins/Hormozi), trust ("brain in a box," "sounds like you").
- **The core differentiation claim:** proactive diagnostic vs. reactive chatbot/ChatGPT.

### Promise-vs-reality gaps the CPO should keep flagging

- **"Turn strangers into high-paying clients" vs. the end-user experience.** Delivery itself is handled (engineers + VAs build all ~50 clients), so the real gap isn't "can we build it" — it's whether the AI chat is *delightful* enough that end-users enroll, stay, and ascend. The current end-user UX feels clunky next to ChatGPT and drives drop-off; closing that gap is the live product work.
- **One offer, not two.** Each client runs one funnel — a low-ticket AI on-ramp that warms cold leads into the client's *existing* high-ticket (Sandra: £17 → £199/mo → £6,000). Don't treat the client's end-user prices as Kodara's pricing — Kodara charges its clients ~$2,500/mo.
- **Ascension benchmarks (10–20% / 5–10%) presented as "typical"** should be treated as hypotheses to validate per client, not established product metrics — and any push toward them must be paired with the trust guardrail (the whole model dies if conversion is juiced at the expense of the expert's reputation).
- **"$100M generated for clients"** is a prior-track-record/marketing claim, not Kodara product evidence — never to be cited as Kodara performance data.
