# Expert-Side Opportunity Solution Tree

Derived from `onboarding-jtbd-2026-06-synthesis.md` (15 onboarding calls, May–June 2026). This is the **expert/buyer** tree. A separate **consumer/lead** tree is still unbuilt — these onboarding calls do not provide consumer-side evidence (see `README.md`).

Every opportunity carries an evidence count (N of 15 clients) so prioritization is by prevalence, not intuition. Counts are from the synthesis; clients can appear under more than one opportunity.

---

## ROOT OUTCOME

**The expert sells and delivers their expertise without being in the room — scaling income past the ceiling set by their own hours and personal presence.**

This is the buyer-side expression of the business's #1 outcome (improve client outcomes). The expert hires Kodara to *recover value they already generate but currently waste*: attention that isn't captured, leads that aren't nurtured, expertise trapped in 1:1 delivery, archives nobody mines, and their own time spent on low-leverage work.

---

## OPPORTUNITIES (customer needs/pains/desires, framed from the expert's view)

Organized as the five "waste" branches. Each is a leak the expert wants closed.

### Branch 1 — "Attention arrives but nothing catches it" (≈6 of 15)
*Robert, Ray, Daron, Dillon, Ashley, Marva.* Top-of-funnel works (content, ads, referrals, viral video) but there's no front-end product to convert attention into a transaction or a captured contact.
- Sub-opportunity: "I need to capture the 95% who aren't ready to buy now" (Robert).
- Sub-opportunity: "I have interest I can't turn into leads" (Ray).

### Branch 2 — "Hand-raisers aren't ready now and never get followed up" (≈6 of 15, overlaps Branch 1)
*Daron, Robert, Dillon + nurture-gap mentions.* The most expensive leak: intent signaled, wrong timing, never touched again.
- Sub-opportunity: "drip/nurture the not-yet-ready until they ascend" (Daron: "if that guy had seen me every week he'd have signed up six months ago").

### Branch 3 — "My expertise only earns when I personally show up" (8 of 15, the core Kodara thesis)
*Bryan, Joe, Tony, Robert, Ashley, Tonja, Facundo, Marva.* Income bolted to their own hours and one-on-one delivery.
- Sub-opportunity: "teach/sell many at once instead of one-on-one" (Bryan, "it's exhausting").
- Sub-opportunity: "eliminate me from the process" (Joe) — held in tension with "but they want the human" (see dissonance below).

### Branch 4 — "I'm sitting on dormant assets nobody is mining" (≈4 of 15)
*Robert (2,000+ past clients in KEAP), Jesse (5,000+ hrs of calls), Joe (4,000 docs), Facundo (scattered archive).* Past value that currently does nothing — both training fuel for the Brain and a re-activatable audience.

### Branch 5 — "I can't build the marketing/lead engine myself" (9 of 15)
*Ashley, Sue, Marva, Daron, Dillon, Milton, Robert, Tonja, Jeremy.* Marketing is the admitted weak spot. This is the most *Kodara-specific* pull: the DFY whole-package including marketing, the thing every client says they cannot do alone.

## THE DOMINANT ANXIETIES (resistance forces — gate every solution against these)

- **Fidelity: "will the AI actually be *me*?" (7 of 15** — Ashley, Bryan, Joe, Milton, Dillon, Facundo, Tonja**).** The #1 trust currency. Note: almost nobody is AI-skeptical *philosophically* — the fear is whether *their* nuance, rapport, and authority survive the transfer.
- **Commercial-model / ownership confusion carried over from the sales handoff (5 of 15** — Robert, Joe, Bryan, Sue, Marva**).**
- **IP / confidentiality as a hard gate (5 of 15** — Marva, Ray, Ashley, Joe (PII), Bryan (HIPAA)**).**
- **Scope / "what exactly am I buying" (3 of 15** — Tonja, Sue, Joe**).**

## CENTRAL PRODUCT TENSION (cognitive dissonance, 5 of 15)

*Joe, Dillon, Bryan, Jeremy, Tonja.* "Replace me / scale me" **vs.** "no one can replace me, they want the human." The thing the expert most wants automated (low/mid-ticket delivery) is the thing they're proudest of and least sure an AI can carry. Resolution direction: design every ladder so AI owns low/mid-ticket and the human is positioned as scarce, premium, high-ticket access.

## HOW TO USE THIS TREE

1. **Make roadmap decisions compare-and-contrast across these opportunities**, not "should we build X." Anchor each to its evidence count.
2. **Gate every solution against the anxieties**, especially fidelity (7/15) and the replace-me-vs-human tension (5/15) — a conversion win that erodes trust violates the Wells-Fargo guardrail.
3. **The handoff de-risk is the keystone.** It's the highest-prevalence opportunity, generalizes across all clients (not a special), and unblocks every other bet. It's the natural lead item for the monthly roadmap.
4. **This is the buyer side only.** Pair it with a consumer-side tree before optimizing the end-user funnel — the synthesis cannot tell you where leads actually drop, hesitate, or churn.
