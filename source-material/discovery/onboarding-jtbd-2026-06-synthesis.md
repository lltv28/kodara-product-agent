## 1. Switch Event Patterns

The triggers cluster into five recurring switch events. Several clients sit in more than one cluster; each client is counted in every cluster they clearly show.

### Cluster A — "Strategic AI-adoption / don't-get-left-behind" pivot, not a crisis (8 of 15)
The largest cluster. These clients were not in acute pain; they made a deliberate, forward-looking bet that AI is the next wave and that not adopting it is itself the risk.
**Clients (8):** Ashley Hobson, Sue Hameed, Tonja Demoff, Joe Hochman, Bryan Burroughs, Jesse Tacoronte, Daron Campbell, Facundo Yebne.
- Ashley: "from what I see, it looks like AI is the way to go moving to the future" / "I want to be ahead of things all the time."
- Sue: "I'm going to go full blown with this business... I feel like it is the next big wave."
- Tonja: "if you don't change and get on board with AI... it's a bad business decision."
- Bryan: "do I want to be... left in the dirt... just being a relic, just not knowing how to use AI. So I figure let's go all in."
- Jesse: father-in-law's "never, ever get behind on technology" / "if I had just put five or 10 grand into Google... I'd be a billionaire."
- Daron: "now is that perfect storm in my mind to teach people."
- Joe: "I'm already sitting at the table, and I'm trying to implement as much AI into my businesses as possible."
- Facundo: AI as workaround for current incapacity, "a clone will be the ideal situation right now."

### Cluster B — Bottleneck of trading time for money / cannot scale themselves (8 of 15)
The core Kodara thesis, showing up as the explicit trigger for half the batch: their income is capped by their own hours and one-on-one delivery.
**Clients (8):** Bryan Burroughs, Joe Hochman, Tony Policci, Robert Weinberg, Ashley Hobson, Tonja Demoff, Facundo Yebne, Marva Allen.
- Bryan: "if I could just teach multiple people at once instead of seeing clients one-on-one... it's exhausting."
- Joe: "If I can only do one client at a time, I can't do 10 others" / "I want to eliminate me from the process."
- Tony: "I'm still trading hours for dollars... I'm time bankrupt."
- Robert: the manual follow-up "hamster wheel" he runs personally.
- Tonja: IP "has always been in my head, one-on-one, like consulting."
- Marva: seven years "heads down building... and delivering our services."

### Cluster C — Lead/demand exists but cannot be converted, nurtured, or monetized (6 of 15)
A working top-of-funnel (organic content, referrals, posts, viral video) that has no front-end product or nurture engine to capture and convert it.
**Clients (6):** Robert Weinberg, Ray Grieves, Daron Campbell, Dillon Dhanecha, Ashley Hobson, Marva Allen.
- Robert: viral YouTube → "I have so many people calling me that aren't ready right now... 95% of those people will never even do a loan. I need to capture that 95%."
- Ray: "there's just a ton of interest... I don't know how to really nurture the interest and turn it into practical leads."
- Daron: "what I haven't done well is... dripped on those people. If that guy had seen me every week... he probably would have signed up six months ago."
- Dillon: partner lead flow decaying, "we now need to decouple from that dependency and build our own lead source."
- Ashley: book/IP "it's there, but nobody's seeing it."

### Cluster D — Revenue plateau, decay, or financial pressure forcing a move (5 of 15)
A measurable downturn, plateau, or near-term financial need.
**Clients (5):** Jeremy Herider, Tony Policci, Dillon Dhanecha, Milton Brown, Robert Weinberg.
- Jeremy: "we're just at a plateau... We get to that like 50, 60 grand a month" and "25, we fell off a cliff" (40% churn).
- Tony: "none of which are bringing in the revenue that I need right now at this phase in my life."
- Dillon: "I need to do 100 grand in June, otherwise I'm [in trouble]."
- Milton: came back to a changed industry, "I had to start the business all over again."

### Cluster E — A specific prior vendor/method failure was the proximate trigger (4 of 15)
A burned-by-the-last-attempt push.
**Clients (4):** Milton Brown, Tony Policci, Robert Weinberg, Bryan Burroughs.
- Milton: Wojo Media — "To say I was disappointed would be an understatement... it took you four months to figure that out."
- Tony: Dean Graziosi equity bet and Copy Avengers defaults ($100K uncollected); two failed AI Summits.
- Robert: course that "I only had one person buy it... that taught me, hey, I really need to prove it."
- Bryan: self-built Trello course that stalled — "It's been a nightmare... that's going to take the rest of this year."

### Strategically important single-appearance trigger
**Marva Allen — investor/fundraising deadline as the buy trigger (1 of 15).** Uniquely, her switch event is funding-driven: "we're out at the investment marketplace, so we need to show first mover advantage." This matters strategically because her success bar is partly *traction-signaling for a raise*, not pure ROI — a different definition of "working" than every other client, and a churn-misread risk if PM measures her only on $17 ROI.

**The literal acquisition channel was a paid ad for at least 9 of 15** (Ashley, Bryan, Ray, Marva, Dillon, Tony, Daron, Jesse, Milton all name seeing/clicking a Kodara/social ad or being converted by Noor's pitch), confirming the front-end ad → Noor pitch motion is doing the demand capture.

## 3. Job Clusters

### Dominant functional jobs
1. **Productize my expertise into a sellable, automated, AI-delivered offer that runs without my hours — typically a low-ticket front end ascending to mid/high ticket (13 of 15).** This is the near-universal functional job. **Clients (13):** Ashley, Robert, Bryan, Ray, Sue, Joe, Tonja, Tony, Marva, Dillon, Milton, Daron, Jesse. (Jeremy and Facundo share the productize-and-scale spirit but their headline jobs are narrower — retention/stickiness for Jeremy, an always-on sales clone for Facundo.)
2. **Build the marketing/lead engine I never built or can't run myself (9 of 15):** Ashley, Sue, Marva, Daron, Dillon, Milton, Robert, Tonja, Jeremy.
3. **Insert a front-end product to capture/nurture/convert demand I already attract (6 of 15):** Robert, Ray, Daron, Dillon, Ashley, Marva.
4. **A clone/avatar that presents and sells as me, 24/7 (7 of 15):** Tony, Bryan, Joe, Facundo, Daron, Jesse, Ashley.

A recurring concrete sub-pattern: a **geo/data-personalized diagnostic as the $17 hero offer** appears in at least 4 of 15 (Robert's DSCR property-scout, Daron's local distressed-deal list, Dillon's social-housing demand analysis, Jesse's credit-report diagnostic; Milton's retirement calculator and Bryan's ADHD assessment are close cousins). This is the most-repeated specific product spec in the batch.

### Most-repeated emotional job
**To feel relevant, ahead-of-the-curve, and not left behind — relief from being the bottleneck (the dominant emotional job, 9 of 15):** Ashley, Bryan, Tonja, Joe, Tony, Facundo, Sue, Jesse, Daron. The single most common emotional through-line is wanting to feel like a forward-moving innovator/visionary rather than obsolete, frequently paired with relief from carrying everything alone. A closely related emotional job — **relief/safety from financial or time pressure** — drives Tony, Dillon, Facundo, Joe, Robert (5).

### Most-repeated social job
**To be seen as the credible, experience-backed authority who stands above a saturated/sleazy field (the dominant social job, 11 of 15):** Ashley ("Ferrari" who can prove it), Bryan ("suicide expert"/"know what your psychiatrist knows"), Joe ("360-degree lawyer," not a "cardboard lawyer"), Tonja ("elite of the elite"/category-of-one), Marva (prestige alternative to exploitative publishers), Milton (trust/credibility with a skeptical demo), Jesse (receipts-backed elder statesman who "shuts down" competitors), Daron (peer of Hormozi/Cardone/Morby), Tony (remembered as a warrior/man of God, not a copywriter), Facundo (legitimate established artist), Robert (the pioneer/"stage expert" of consumer mortgage AI). Differentiation-from-the-undifferentiated-mass is named explicitly by Ashley, Tonja, Marva, Jesse, Joe, Milton.

### Cognitive-dissonance pattern showing up in more than one call
**Pattern 1 — "Stated modest/commercial reason vs. underlying legacy/identity/ambition driver" (8 of 15):** Ashley ("just cover costs" vs. world-stage legacy), Bryan (altruistic teaching vs. revenue/status), Tony (revenue-first vs. legacy/meaning), Jesse (recurring revenue vs. resolving the Google regret/legacy), Joe (eliminate-me vs. pride in being the human), Tonja (strategic opportunity vs. fear-of-obsolescence), Robert (lead-nurture moat vs. $500K exit from mortgages), Marva (ROI vs. investor-signaling). **PM implication: do not take the stated success bar at face value for these clients; the real Job is bigger and more identity-loaded than the words.**

**Pattern 2 — "I want the AI to replace me / scale me" vs. "no one can replace me / they want the human" (5 of 15):** Joe ("eliminate me" vs. "at high ticket they want Joe Hochman... the human"), Dillon ("AI can do exactly what I'm doing" vs. "no one can replace me on the one day"), Bryan (scale beyond one-on-one vs. rapport is "the very thing that makes me effective"), Jeremy ("you guys get it all done" vs. "it's the face-to-face, the human contact"), Tonja (full DFY vs. "only involved at the high-ticket"). This is the central product tension of the cohort: the thing they most want automated is the thing they're proudest of and least sure an AI can carry.

**Pattern 3 — "I'm not a coach / it's not consulting" vs. selling exactly that (2 of 15):** Ray ("I don't want to be a coach. I'm not a coach" while selling system-access education) and, in milder form, Milton ("not completely above board" workshop method vs. wanting to feel clean/direct).

## 4. Outcomes & Expectation Gaps

### Outcomes clients most commonly imagine
1. **A low-ticket front end that ascends buyers into mid/high-ticket revenue without their manual effort (13 of 15 — see Section 3).** Nearly everyone pictures the ladder.
2. **A specific revenue number or ROI threshold as the win (8 of 15):** Robert ("$500,000... net income," six figures in 12 months), Joe ("a million dollars in year one," then "a million a month"; "pays for itself in month one"), Dillon ("100 grand in June," "a million pounds"), Marva ("$3,500 back in $17 signups"), Daron ("sell 50 a day," "stack a million"), Facundo ("$20,000, $30,000 a month"), Tonja ($40K flagship ladder), Milton (annuity ascensions, his real revenue). Ashley uniquely sets a *deliberately low* bar ("just cover costs") that masks bigger ambition.
3. **An always-on clone/avatar that sells while they sleep (7 of 15):** Tony, Bryan, Joe, Facundo, Daron, Jesse, Ashley.
4. **A fast quick-win / "light-bulb" experience for the end buyer (named by Sue, Joe, Milton, Daron, Jeremy — 5).**

### Where client timeline expectations diverge from delivery reality
Kodara set a near-identical delivery cadence on essentially every call: **preview in ~2–3 days, version 1.0 in 14–18 days (worst case ~30), then iterate.** This was stated to all 15. Against that:

- **Build-timeline alignment is broadly good (no client pushed back on 14–18 days).** The divergence is not about the *build* clock; it is about the **results/ROI clock layered on top of it.**
- **The most dangerous, quantifiable gap — Joe Hochman:** his win condition is "if this investment pays for itself in month one, and month two it duplicates or triplicates," but Lucas explicitly decoupled revenue from month 1 and named "a four to six week testing period" before a winning angle emerges. A normal month-1 outcome will read to Joe as failure. This is the clearest expectation mismatch in the batch.
- **Daron Campbell:** expects "50 a day / 400 people" against his own real track record of 72 mentorships from 2,000 downloads over 90 days. The build will ship on time; the *conversion* expectation outruns demonstrated math.
- **Dillon Dhanecha:** a hard external "100 grand in June" deadline against a 14–18 day v1.0 leaves a thin margin for the build to actually move the June number — speed is aligned but the results-by-deadline expectation is tight.
- **Inverse risk (long runway lowers urgency) — Ashley, Tonja, Robert:** Ashley ("I've got another 10 years"), Tonja (real deadline is the Aug 29 TEDx), Robert (patient 12-month proof window). Here the gap is the opposite — the vendor drives timeline and client urgency can drift.
- **Sue Hameed and Milton Brown explicitly track the date** (Sue is "super type A"; Milton double-checked "mid-June" twice). For them any preview slip reads as a broken promise even though the stated timeline matches.

**Quantified pattern:** of the 8 clients who attached a revenue number to "success," at least 3 (Joe, Daron, Dillon) hold a results-timeline that is more aggressive than what was promised, while 3 others (Ashley, Tonja, Robert) hold one that is *slower* than the build cadence and risks drift.

### Expectation gaps most likely to create disappointment at scale
1. **ROI-by-month-one vs. "4–6 week testing period before a winner emerges" (highest revenue impact).** Affects the highest-paying, highest-ambition clients (Joe at $25K, Daron, Dillon, Robert's $500K, Tonja's $40K ladder). If results-timeline expectations aren't reset *in writing*, premium accounts will judge a normal ramp as failure — directly at the 30-day refund window (Joe, Marva, Sue all reference the refund/first-month checkpoint).
2. **"The AI will fully be me" vs. v1.0 is "good but not perfect" (7 of 15, Section 2 fidelity anxiety).** The first preview must visibly quote their own material/voice back or the fidelity-anxious clients lose conviction.
3. **Ascension gap — buyers nibble the low ticket but don't climb (named explicitly for Milton, Daron, Tonja, Joe).** Clients whose real revenue is the high ticket (Milton's annuity, Joe's $25K, Tonja's $40K) will call the engagement a failure if low-ticket buyers don't ascend, regardless of front-end signups.

## 5. Portfolio Stall-Risk View

### Distribution and batch average
| Score | Count | Clients |
|-------|-------|---------|
| 1 (lowest) | 0 | — |
| 2 | 7 | Robert Weinberg, Sue Hameed, Dillon Dhanecha, Jeremy Herider, Milton Brown, Daron Campbell, Jesse Tacoronte |
| 3 | 6 | Ashley Hobson, Bryan Burroughs, Ray Grieves, Joe Hochman, Tonja Demoff, Marva Allen |

| 5 (highest) | 0 | — |

**Batch average stall-risk score: 2.67 / 5** (sum 40 ÷ 15). The cohort is moderate-risk and centrally clustered (no 1s, no 5s); nobody is a sure thing and nobody is written off.

### Highest-risk clients and the factor each shares
The two highest-risk clients are **Tony Policci (4/5)** and **Facundo Yebne (4/5).**
- **Tony:** no settled offer/niche + chronic-overthinker with a lifelong fragmented-focus pattern; the risk is *decision-and-follow-through*, not capacity.
- **Facundo:** severe personal unavailability (sleeping a few hours a week, active litigation, self-admittedly unresponsive) + open offer decision + scattered data mid-migration.

**Shared factor:** both have an **unresolved offer/direction decision AND a follow-through/availability vulnerability** — the build cannot start cleanly and the client may go quiet at the wrong moment. That same pairing (open decision + handoff dependency) is exactly what pulls the 3-scored clients up too.

### Early-warning signal correlating with higher stall risk across multiple calls
**The single strongest cross-call early-warning signal: the build is gated on a content/data/login handoff that depends entirely on the client (or their team), and the client is busy, overloaded, or has fuzzy handoff mechanics.** This appears in 13 of 15 reports as a named stall risk: Ashley, Robert, Bryan, Ray, Sue, Joe, Tonja, Marva, Dillon, Facundo, Milton, Jesse, Jeremy. The two clients where it's weakest are also the lowest practical risk on this axis (Daron and Jesse are asset-rich and overnight-responsive — though Jesse's *real* corpus is staged separately). **The handoff dependency is the universal stall vector; everything else (offer indecision, ROI mismatch) is secondary.**

A second, narrower early-warning signal: **an unresolved offer/avatar/positioning decision left open at the end of the call** correlates with the higher scores — present for Tony (4), Facundo (4), Ray (3), Joe (3), Tonja (3), and Jesse (the credit-vs-funding question). When the offer is undefined *and* the handoff depends on the client, risk concentrates.

A third signal worth flagging for PM: **a results-timeline expectation more aggressive than the promised 4–6 week testing period** (Joe, Daron, Dillon) is a *post-launch* stall/churn vector even for otherwise-low-risk clients — it doesn't show in the build phase but threatens the renewal/refund window.

### Onboarding friction points that repeat across calls
1. **New-tool / Slack aversion — 6 of 15:** Ashley ("What is Slack?"), Marva ("I have not used Slack"), Bryan (used Slack "for a month"), Facundo (rejected Slack over notification failure → WhatsApp), Jeremy ("Three clicks for me and you lost me"), Dillon (deleted his stack, must re-add Slack). **Recommendation: default to the lightest familiar channel (WhatsApp for Ashley/Facundo), one channel not five, one-click approvals.**
2. **Sales-to-onboarding handoff gaps: missing contract / fuzzy commercial model — 4 of 15:** Marva (missing agreement, raised twice), Sue (missing contract + email in spam), Bryan (contract maybe in junk), Robert (re-asked ownership/rental/annual/royalty left fuzzy by the sales rep). **Recommendation: send signed agreement + a plain-English "exactly what you bought" one-pager within 24h on every account.**
3. **Scope / "what do you actually do / one-stop-shop" confusion — 3 of 15:** Tonja ("it might be easier for you to tell me what you don't do"), Sue, Joe ("there's a lot of blur with what exactly your product is"). **Recommendation: a day-one "what we do / don't do / need from you" scope + tool-ownership sheet.**
4. **Content/data handoff mechanics unclear or client-built — 5 of 15:** Milton ("I couldn't figure that out" re: Google Drive), Ray (ChatGPT login), Dillon (path spread across GHL/Drive/WhatsApp), Facundo (scattered/mid-migration), Jesse (sprawling team, 50-person Slack). **Recommendation: a single dead-simple intake checklist and pull data for the client wherever possible.**
5. **The offer/avatar decision left open at call's end — 5 of 15:** Tony, Facundo, Ray, Jesse, Joe (and Daron's $17 "one thing"). **Recommendation: the first deliverable should present a decision to confirm, not open questions.**

### Trust & confidence signals (for Trust Report / Brain Health dashboard)
- **Fidelity / "does it sound like me / quote my real material" is the #1 trust currency — 7 of 15** (Ashley, Bryan, Joe, Milton, Dillon, Facundo, Tonja, plus Jeremy who QAs by "does it sound like me?"). The Trust Report should foreground **source provenance** (their book, their years, named mentors, their own calls) so answers feel experience-backed, and Brain Health should show **coverage of their frameworks** with gap-flagging. Multiple clients volunteered to record video to fill gaps (Ashley, Bryan).
- **"Only answers from your data / won't hallucinate" reframed as a trust feature lands well — 3 of 15** (Ray, Facundo, Jesse explicitly responded to it). Keep this front-and-center in Brain Health.
- **IP isolation / "you don't train on my files" is a hard trust gate — 5 of 15** (Marva "don't feed AI our proprietary system," Ray PII scrubbing, Ashley IP question, Joe confidentiality/PII redaction for a legal client, Bryan HIPAA). Brain Health should surface which assets are ingested vs. internal-only/excluded. **Note the open liability:** for Joe, Lucas conceded redaction is "sometimes not 100%, our team corrects it manually" — a trust risk for a lawyer unless framed as a rigorous verifiable workflow.
- **Freshness / "last updated / source recency" — 2 of 15** (Robert: regulatory content "could change every day"; Bryan: prune pre-2022). A Brain Health recency view directly answers a stated anxiety.
- **Warm-vs-cold-traffic validity — 1 but strategically sharp** (Dillon: "people that know me are testing it... not quite accurate. We should do it... with cold traffic"). A Trust Report distinguishing warm vs. cold performance would resonate.
- **"Founder shows up / is real" as a trust anchor — 2 of 15** (Jeremy paid $2,500 literally to test if the founder shows up; Tony's high personal trust in Lucas). Mirror-able in onboarding.

### Systemic stall risks the whole cohort shares
1. **Client-gated content/data handoff is the universal launch dependency (13 of 15).** This is the cohort's #1 systemic risk. Systemic fix: every account gets a single one-channel intake checklist within 24h, Kodara pulls data wherever possible, and a dated first micro-action is requested while motivation is high.
2. **A results-timeline expectation more aggressive than the 4–6 week testing window threatens the 30-day refund/renewal window** for the highest-paying accounts (Joe, Daron, Dillon, plus refund-checkpoint references from Marva, Sue). Systemic fix: reset ROI expectations in writing on every account, anchored to a realistic ramp + the levers Kodara controls (nurture, financing).
3. **The "fully replace me vs. they want the human" tension (5 of 15)** is unresolved cohort-wide and will surface as conviction loss if v1 feels impersonal. Systemic fix: design every ladder so AI owns low/mid ticket and the human is positioned as scarce, premium, high-ticket access — and make v1 previews visibly carry the founder's voice.

1. **De-risk the client-gated content/data handoff on every account — it is the universal stall vector. (Affects 13 of 15: Ashley, Robert, Bryan, Ray, Sue, Joe, Tonja, Marva, Dillon, Facundo, Milton, Jesse, Jeremy.)** Ship a single, dead-simple, one-channel intake checklist within 24 hours, pull data for the client wherever possible (logins, public sources, live screen-share), and convert enthusiasm into one dated micro-action while motivation is high. 2. **Reset the results-timeline expectation in writing on every premium account, decoupling revenue from the 14–18 day build and naming the 4–6 week testing window. (Affects the highest-revenue accounts most acutely: Joe $25K, Daron, Dillon, Robert's $500K goal, Tonja's $40K ladder; refund-checkpoint references from Marva and Sue.)** Joe's "pays for itself in month one" and Daron's "50 a day" against his real 72/2,000 history are disappointment-at-the-refund-window waiting to happen. (Affects 7 of 15: Ashley, Bryan, Joe, Milton, Dillon, Facundo, Tonja.)** The first preview must visibly quote the client's own material and voice back accurately, show source provenance (their book, years, mentors, calls), flag framework-coverage gaps, and keep the "only answers from your data, won't hallucinate" behavior front-and-center.
