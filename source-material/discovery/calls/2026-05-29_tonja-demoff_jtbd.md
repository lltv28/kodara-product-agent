## 1. The Switch Event

The trigger is less a single breaking-point crisis and more a deliberate, strategic pivot by an experienced operator who has decided the cost of *not* adopting AI is now a business risk. Tonja frames this as a forward-looking bet, not a desperate rescue.

**What was happening right before she reached out:**
She is mid-rebuild of her business, deliberately relaunching her body of work under a new thesis (regulated nervous system as the engine of sustainable wealth). Much of the IP is still being assembled because it has only ever lived in her head as one-on-one consulting.
> "a lot of it is being built because it's always been in my head, one-on-one, like consulting and things. So a lot of it's being built at the same time, which is what I'm excited about." (lines 563-564)

She has a hard external deadline forcing momentum: an Aug 29 TEDx talk she wants marketing built *around* and *before*.
> "I think if there's something out before the TED Talk and everything, that's smarter than waiting till after it." (line 600)

**The breaking point / moment of frustration:**
There is no acute current crisis. The "push" is strategic fear of obsolescence plus the recognition that her entire marketing skill set is from a prior era.
> "if you don't change and get on board with AI, I just think you're going to be way behind the curve. And I think it's a bad business decision to not understand AI and take advantage of it." (lines 18-19)
> "I've built all my businesses on the old way of marketing... LA Times advertisements, business journals... different forms of direct mail marketing." (lines 21-22)

The deeper, historical breaking point that shapes *what she now sells* (not why she hired Kodara) is her own lawsuit, default judgment, and near-bankruptcy:
> "I received a default judgment from the attorney's negligence... Multi-million dollar judgment that I then appealed and I won at the appeal level. But by that time I was broke." (lines 101-105)

**What she tried before and why it fell short:**
- Old-media marketing (print, direct mail): still works for her but doesn't reach people "faster, easier... immediately" and doesn't match where her buyers now are (lines 22-24).
- Self-serve AI tinkering with Replit: she was "just playing around" and ran a website analysis, but explicitly positions herself as non-technical and unable to build it herself.
  > "I am not super techie, okay?... I don't know anything about building it behind the scenes." (lines 592-595)
- A GoHighLevel contractor ("my co-high-level guy") who has *talked* about AI voice/podcast capability but apparently hasn't delivered it, leaving her unsure what is already covered.
  > "My co-high-level guy has been talking to me about training AI to use my voice to do podcasts... Can this AI do that?" (lines 606-607)

## 3. The Functional, Emotional, and Social Jobs

**Functional job:**
Productize expertise that currently lives only in her head into a sellable, AI-delivered ecosystem with a multi-tier funnel, and have a partner run the modern marketing she cannot.
> "the whole reason for me doing this is to get to the high-ticket and have a sales process that supports that." (lines 454-455)
> "I want to align everything I'm doing with the kind of offer that's going to help you guys... you can say, hey, this offer would work better." (lines 586-588)
Plus a concrete sub-job: turn ~100 existing podcast scripts into AI-voiced/AI-avatar output (lines 606-614).

**Emotional job:**
To feel ahead of the curve rather than left behind, to feel like a smart strategic operator making "good decisions with good companies," and to escape the lonely, image-protecting burden of doing everything herself.
> "That's why now for me is I've never done it. It's exciting." (line 28)
> "most entrepreneurs have one thing they're really good at and everything else they need a team that knows how to do it to come in and help them." (line 363)
This is the same emotional driver she sells *into* her own clients (relief from the dysregulation of having to hold it all together), which makes her unusually self-aware about it.

**Social job:**
To be seen as a category-of-one thought leader who created a *new* connection nobody else has made (regulated nervous system → sustainable wealth), validated by the TEDx stage, and to be perceived as elite/high-ticket rather than commoditized.
> "I don't even see anybody doing what I'm doing, which is making the connection... nobody else is making that." (lines 217, 249)
> "it really has to be positioned as the elite of the elite do this." (line 507)
> TEDx as social proof: "One Betrayal Away From Broke... it'll be at Jefferson University... on the main TED channel." (lines 535-543)

**Cognitive dissonance flagged:**
1. **"Strategic AI adoption" vs. fear-of-obsolescence.** Her stated reason is upside ("opportunity to reach people faster, easier, immediately," line 24), but the underlying driver is loss-avoidance: not wanting to be "way behind the curve" and judging non-adoption as "a bad business decision" (18-19). The real motivator is fear of being left behind, dressed as opportunity.
2. **Anti-victim identity vs. her own teaching.** She teaches that high achievers must admit "I've been victimized" (line 372), yet narrates her own instinct to hide it: "I just felt, hide that... I don't want that out there" (line 357). She sells the cure for a wound she still partly carries, which is a powerful marketing asset but also a personal sensitivity to handle carefully.
3. **"It has to be a collaboration / I'm open to anything" vs. tight control.** She invites Kodara to redesign her offer ("you can say, hey, this offer would work better," 588) but simultaneously sets non-negotiables (only personally involved at high-ticket, 452) and wants final say on content (647). Openness is real but bounded.

## 5. Product Insights for PM

**Feature gaps or requests (implied or stated):**
- **AI voice + AI avatar podcast generation from existing scripts.** Directly requested. She has ~100 scripts and just bought Riverside equipment.
  > "Can this AI do that?... right now I've got about 100 podcasts. Scripts already." (607-608). Lucas confirmed avatar/voice is doable "later next week" (616-619). PM action: make the avatar/voice deliverable explicit in her plan and confirm it can ingest pre-written scripts in bulk.
- **A "Wealth Archetype" quiz/assessment as a funnel mechanism.** She already designed archetypes and a test; this is a natural lead-magnet/router the AI brain should operationalize.
  > "a person would take a test, find out this is your wealth archetype... we call that your wealth map." (265, 338). PM action: build the archetype quiz as the top-of-funnel router that segments leads into the right tier/track.
- **Qualification gate / disqualification copy at the high-ticket entry.** She admired a funnel that screens by price tolerance.
  > "if that's not something you can do... then wait until you can... that's fabulous." (510-511). PM action: add an explicit price-qualifier ("here's an alternative to get started") to protect high-ticket positioning.
- **Branching funnel logic (Entrepreneur vs. Real Estate Investment).** The $40k flagship forks; the AI router must support two downstream tracks (lines 472-476).
- **Source-tagging by angle.** Lucas already framed this; the AI should route based on entry angle (legal trauma vs. divorce vs. tax) — lines 482-487.

**Onboarding friction points:**
- **Scope confusion ("one-stop shop?").** Repeatedly unsure what Kodara does vs. what she still must source. This is the single most repeated question in the call.
  > "are we a one-stop shop here or what do I need?... it might be easier for you to tell me what you don't do than what you do." (678-686). PM action: deliver a one-page "what we do / what we don't do / what we need from you" scope sheet in Slack on day one.
- **Tool-overlap / duplication anxiety.** She fears building things twice (GHL contractor, Opus Clip, her own social).
  > "I don't want to have one thing being created over here if it's something you guys are already... planning for." (628-629). PM action: produce a tool-ownership matrix mapping each existing tool/vendor to "Kodara handles / you handle / collaborate."
- **Non-technical reviewer.** She needs simplified, well-explained deliverables to approve quickly.
  > "the more user-friendly you send something to me with a little more clear explanation... the faster I can give it back to you." (598). PM action: annotate every preview with plain-language context and an explicit "what we need from you" ask.
- **Paid-traffic uncertainty.** She doesn't know where to advertise and Kodara doesn't push ad-account buttons; this is an unowned seam.
  > "I wouldn't know where to market it right now... as far as paid advertising." (701) and Lucas: "we don't actually go in and... click the actual settings to control the budget" (688). PM action: clarify the ads-execution handoff (her VA / GHL guy / Kodara AI) before launch so it doesn't stall at go-live.

**Trust & confidence signals (relevant to Trust Report / Brain Health dashboard):**
- She repeatedly probes whether Ricardo is real or AI ("Are you really you, Ricardo, or are you an AI?" 38; "you're not making this AI right now, are you?" 749). She is curious and slightly testing the realism/honesty of AI — a signal that an AI-version-of-her must clear a high believability bar and that transparency about what's AI matters to her trust.
- She references Kodara's guarantee as a condition of her bigger white-label vision: "if this goes the way you guys guarantee" (705). The guarantee is load-bearing for her expansion intent; Brain Health/Trust Report progress should visibly track against it.

**Stall risks:**

- **Long runway to her real deadline.** Her urgency is pinned to late August; weeks of low pressure could let momentum drift.

- **High-ticket-only personal involvement** means the low/mid tiers must be carried almost entirely by AI; if those tiers underwhelm, she may disengage since she "wouldn't take it today" herself (498-501).

## 6. Stall Risk Score

**Score: 3 / 5 (moderate).**

She is highly motivated, financially capable, decisive, and already collaborating on tooling, which pulls risk down; but her core product IP is still being built from scratch ("it's always been in my head," 563), her real urgency is pinned to a late-August TEDx rather than now, and there is an unresolved division of labor with her GoHighLevel contractor plus open scope/duplication confusion, all of which can quietly delay content handoff and launch. These are dependency-and-momentum risks, not commitment risks.

**Recommended intervention:** Within 24-48 hours, deliver a single plain-language "scope + ownership + what-we-need-from-you" sheet in the new Slack channel (covering exactly what Kodara does/doesn't do, the tool-ownership matrix, and a short content-collection checklist), then set one concrete pre-TEDx milestone date so her August deadline starts pulling work forward now instead of in August.
