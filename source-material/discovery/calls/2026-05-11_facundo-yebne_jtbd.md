# JTBD Analysis: Facundo Yebne Onboarding Call (2026-05-11)

Client: Facundo Yebne (Miami-based artist, "Fly Art Miami" / duck-based UV/glow art; former 20+ year Airbnb/short-term-rental operator)
Call: Kodara onboarding call with Lucas Lee-Tyson, following an earlier discovery conversation with Noor
Two candidate offers discussed: (1) an Airbnb/short-term-rental consulting AI, and (2) an art-brand AI clone to sell and represent his artwork. Client and Lucas both lean toward the art offer by call's end.

---

## 1. The Switch Event

**What was happening right before he reached out:** Facundo went from running a large operation to being effectively a solo operator, and is simultaneously consumed by a brutal legal fight. He sold his management company two years ago and his laundry business last year, losing the staff who carried his institutional knowledge.

> "When the management company, I went from having 200 employees to have my assistant, and when I sold the laundry last year, she stayed with that company, and I found myself with a lousy assistant and AI and a lot of that, so I relay a lot on the work of AI"

**The breaking point / frustration:** A combination of personal trauma and an active lawsuit has made him unable to do the public-facing, in-person pitching that built his success. The clone is framed explicitly as a workaround for his current incapacity.

> "The past few years have been kind of bad in the way of people dying, like family, partners stealing from me. Right now, I'm dealing with a lawsuit against a building..."

> "Right now, if I had to go out and pitch somebody, it's not going to happen... I'm very conscious of that situation, which has been going on for so long that a clone will be the ideal situation right now."

> "Since December that I sleep maybe a few hours a week."

**The recurring AI pain that made him seek a real product:** He already uses AI heavily (Gamma, Claude Code), but he could not get it to be persistent and cumulative; he had to rebuild context every time.

> "I relay a lot on the work of AI, but I couldn't do something that was steady and collective that It grew with the information that I provided, but I had to, like, do everything again every time."

**What he tried before:**
- DIY AI tools (Gamma for the Miami Beach pitch deck, Claude Code on his desktop) - worked for one-off tasks but not as a durable, growing brain.
- A "lousy assistant" after losing his trained staff - insufficient.
- A real estate/Airbnb empire and a one-man art operation - both bottlenecked on *him* personally being present.

## 3. The Functional, Emotional, and Social Jobs

**Functional job:** Turn his expertise and brand into an always-on AI that can present, sell, and answer for him without requiring him to be physically present or mentally available. For art: a clone that introduces people to his brand, tells his story, and sells. He also surfaced a second functional job: a private thinking/operations partner.

> "to adapt the person itself, and then they can download the image, or I can do a 3D print" (visualization feature on the art funnel)

> "Can I use it for something else, maybe? Like checking when I do projects and I leave Claude running something, to be checking on that?"

**Emotional job:** Relief and a return to control while he is overwhelmed, sleepless, and under siege. He wants the business to keep moving without draining the energy he no longer has.

> "I need that because some days I can't think straight with all this noise."

> "let's build a clone that has my good looks and my good energy, and let's kick [it] with that."

**Social job:** To be perceived as a legitimate, established artist despite only "a year and a half of being an artist," and to be seen the way the art world demands - through who represents you and the prestige around you. He wants the clone to project the credibility his accolades earned (Miami Beach commission, Florence Biennale award) and put him in the league of self-made art brands like Romero Britto.

> "since the art world moves by who has your [representation]... even though I have two really amazing things... I have a year and a half of being an artist. That is not good enough."

> "Do you know who Romero Brito is?... he is without a gallery... he doesn't need them to do it." (his model of success)

**Cognitive dissonance flag:** His *stated* business logic favors the Airbnb offer ("the easiest of the two... I know it from even in my sleep"), but his *underlying driver* is clearly the art - it's his identity, his passion, and his escape from a real estate world that now causes him pain.

> "Art... It's my new passion. The Airbnb got to a point that I didn't enjoy it anymore."

> "the building that I... made myself millionaires. In that building, but right now it's killing me."

> "let's push for the art one."

The dissonance is "safe choice (Airbnb expertise) vs. true want (art)." By call's end the dissonance resolves toward art, with Lucas validating it. PM should treat the art offer as the real job-to-be-done.

> "Software-wise... it's a phase of first shape the content... then doing the programming, testing... those are the main things to do."

> "Usually, we can get, like, the version 1.0 done inside of 14, 18 days."

## 5. Product Insights for PM

**Feature gaps / requests implied or stated:**
- *Persistent, cumulative brain* is the core unmet need from prior tools - the product must visibly demonstrate it "grows with the information" rather than resetting. > "I couldn't do something that was steady and collective that It grew with the information that I provided"
- *Image personalization / visualization feature* on the art funnel (user uploads a photo, art adapts to them, downloadable or 3D-printable). > "the idea is for people to upload their picture, for this to be, to adapt the person itself, and then they can download the image, or I can do a 3D print."
- *Private operator/thought-partner mode* beyond the customer-facing salesperson. > "Like checking when I do projects and I leave Claude running something, to be checking on that?"
- *Website embed + subdomain architecture* alongside his Shopify store (ai.flyartmiami.x as a funnel; clone embedded/linked on the homepage to capture organic + press traffic). > "Will it be... the AI to be on the homepage and grabbing people also coming organically to the website?"

**Onboarding friction points:**
- *Unresolved offer decision* - the single most important onboarding output (art vs. Airbnb) was deferred to a post-call team review. Lucas committed to a recommendation within 24 hours. This is a friction point only if that decision slips.
- *Data is scattered* across computer, office desktop, and Google Drive; he is mid-migration to a single server. He insists collection is fast ("half a day"), but the real bottleneck is server/upload speed. > "the data is... all scattered around, but I can, whatever the two of them that you choose, it's half a day."
- *The art positioning copy was AI-generated and unreviewed by him* - he wants to re-read it before it reaches the marketing team. Risk of building on positioning that doesn't match his intent. > "I didn't go through the final copy that I see in the link, so I would like to repeat it before you send your team."
- *Channel friction* - he rejected Slack due to a past notification failure; team aligned on WhatsApp, which he uses "all day." Good resolution, but the underlying issue (he misses messages) persists.

**Trust and confidence signals (relevant to Trust Report / Brain Health):**
- He explicitly trusts Kodara's track record: "it's going to work because you guys have the proof." This is a confidence asset to reinforce.
- He worries the brain will answer beyond its knowledge; Lucas reframed the "only knows what you feed it" constraint as a *trust feature* (no hallucinated answers). This maps directly to a Trust Report / Brain Health value prop. > "if you ask it something that we have not fed it... it will literally tell you, like, I don't know how to answer that."
- His Brain Health depends on ingesting a messy, possibly redundant, multi-year archive - a Brain Health dashboard flagging gaps/redundancy would resonate with his stated concern: "maybe it's repeated, maybe it's irrelevant."

**Stall risks:**
- *Personal availability* is the dominant risk. He is sleep-deprived, in active litigation, and self-describes as unresponsive. He pre-emptively asked the team to chase him. > "if you send me an email and I don't respond, to let me know... I'm so out of it some days."
- *He is a parallel builder* (own Shopify site, own press releases, own AI stack), which can create scope confusion or duplicated/conflicting work if not coordinated.
- *Pending self-review of art copy* could delay marketing kickoff.
- *Mitigating factor:* he calls this "for me a priority" and is "very excited," and the live June show creates urgency that pulls against stall.

**Messaging / positioning language to mirror in marketing:**
- > "let's build a clone that has my good looks and my good energy" - personality-forward clone framing.
- > "a clone will be the ideal situation right now" - clone-as-presence-when-you-can't-be-there.
- > "have you ever seen a happy duck?" - his own brand hook; the art's emotional trigger of childhood/happiness is ready-made copy. > "everybody triggers a good feeling, a good memory, something related to happiness."
- "Create the unexpected" / "something that nobody ever saw before" - his differentiation language for the UV/glow duck art.
- The salesperson-for-every-visitor frame Lucas used landed well and can be reused: a website AI that "can introduce them, can answer questions... And then if it needs to sell them something, they can do that as well."

## 6. Stall Risk Score

**Score: 4 / 5 (elevated stall risk).**

Justification: The client is genuinely motivated, trusts Kodara, has a hard live catalyst (June 4 Epic Hotel show), and says the work is a priority, which all pull toward launch. However, he is severely time- and attention-constrained (sleeping a few hours a week, deep in litigation, self-admittedly unresponsive), the offer decision and his own art-copy review are still open, and his data is scattered mid-migration - any one of which can stall momentum if he goes quiet at the wrong moment.

Recommended intervention: Move all communication to the WhatsApp group immediately and assign one owner to drive async, decision-by-default check-ins (e.g., "Unless you object by X, we proceed with the art offer and this copy"), so progress never waits on his initiative. Anchor the entire build timeline to the June 4 show as the visible deadline.

## Top 3 Takeaways for the PM Team (ranked)

1. **Build the art offer, not the Airbnb offer.** Both client and Lucas land on art; it is his passion, identity, and escape from the real estate world that now "kills" him, and it carries unique differentiation (UV/glow duck art, Miami Beach commission, Florence Biennale award). The Airbnb pitch is his "safe" rationalization, not his true job-to-be-done. > "let's push for the art one."

2. **Engineer around his unavailability from day one.** His single biggest risk is going dark while sleep-deprived and litigating. He explicitly asked to be chased. Default to WhatsApp, one owner, decision-by-default check-ins, and a self-driving cadence that does not require his initiative. > "if you send me an email and I don't respond, to let me know."

3. **Time the v1 to the June 4 Epic Hotel show and deliver the persistent-brain proof early.** Use the live show as the launch deadline and the press-release moment as the traffic driver, and in the first 2-3 day preview, demonstrate the one thing prior AI tools failed at: a brain that grows and persists, plus the "only answers from your data" trust behavior. > "I couldn't do something that was steady and collective that It grew with the information that I provided."
