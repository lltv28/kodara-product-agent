## 1. The Switch Event

**What was happening right before he reached out.** Ashley is a 60-year-old, 40+ year tennis coach with a strong word-of-mouth business that "does well," but he is hitting a life-stage inflection point rather than a business emergency. The trigger is a combination of (a) a clear empty-nest horizon and (b) a body of intellectual property (book, pyramid framework, presentations) that he feels is sitting unseen.

The life-stage trigger is explicit:
> "And I've got two kids, and one kid's about to go to college, the next kid is about two years out from going to college, and then I've got an empty home, so I'm like, okay, what's the next stage going to be, so I'm thinking if I can get my knowledge out there, get more... opportunity to speak and help the development of tennis, I think this would be great."

The "it's sitting unseen" trigger is repeated several times:
> "I brought out a book last November, and I'd like to get that to be a little bit more, just put it out there more, you know."
> "So that's kind of like it's there, but nobody's seeing it, you know?"

**The breaking point / moment of frustration.** There is no acute pain or crisis. The closest thing to friction is self-directed frustration that marketing has always been his weak spot, and a sense that he discovered this gap late:
> "my marketing side has always been the worst side of me, you know. And kind of partly because I've never really chased numbers."
> "I've just been really a blue-collar guy working every day, you know, in the trenches. So I haven't done enough marketing, and I'm finding this too late in life, really, I suppose. But, you know, I think I've got another 10 years."

A secondary irritant is being asked to share expertise for free at federation/club events:
> "a lot of us, they expect you to do it for free because they're inviting you to speak in front of all these people. And I'm like, it's still taking me away from my business here... it kind of, a little bit irks me that you don't get paid for stuff like that."

**What he had tried before and why it fell short.** Organic social and a tiny, neglected ad spend, run on autopilot with no real attention:
> "we've got Instagram, which I probably feed every single day... got YouTube, which I really haven't looked after well enough."
> "I've got an ad running right now, too. So, I give, you know, a couple of hundred bucks a month to it, I don't even know. I look at it, like, every six months, and then update it."

He has also done in-person clinics/consulting for major federations and clubs, but never packaged or marketed it as a product:
> "I've done clinics around the world to different federations and clubs."
> "I've kind of, like, been very, like, if you ask me, I'll come, but I haven't marketed it out there."

## 3. The Functional, Emotional, and Social Jobs

**Functional job (the practical task).** Package and distribute his existing expertise (book, nine-stage performance pyramid, PowerPoint presentations, 40 years of method) into a marketed, online, productized form that reaches people beyond word-of-mouth and generates at least cost-covering revenue, with a clear secondary functional job of opening a consulting / speaking / coach-education line.
> "I'd like to do is just maybe get into more consulting and get more opportunities to do that. That's kind of my interest now."
> "if I can get my knowledge out there, get more... opportunity to speak and help the development of tennis."

**Emotional job (how he wants to feel).** He wants to feel innovative, ahead of the curve, and legacy-secured (relevant, purposeful in his next decade). The money is explicitly secondary; the feeling of being a forward-moving innovator is primary.
> "I like to try and think of myself as being pretty innovative... I want to be ahead of things all the time."
> "if this is the future, I want to be a part of it."
> "I'll die working, but I don't consider this work... it's a pleasure."

**Social job (how he wants to be perceived).** He wants to be seen as the credible, experience-backed authority who stands apart from the undifferentiated mass of self-proclaimed elite coaches, and recognized on a world stage (not just locally). He wants to be the "Ferrari" who can actually prove it, and the mentor whose method gets passed down.
> "everybody sounds and says that they're, you know, they're Ferraris. And so it's tough to break through all the chatter."
> "my mentors were the best in the world... I worked for three out of four... I'm just slightly revamping what they did and taking it to another level. And hopefully in the future, somebody will take what I have and improve upon it."
> "one of the reasons I moved here was, I wanted to prove myself against the biggest... place in the world" (IMG).

**Cognitive dissonance flag.** There is a clear stated-vs-underlying mismatch. His stated reason is modest and money-minimizing ("I just want to cover my costs," "I'm not chasing numbers," "I'm more chasing quality"). His underlying drivers, surfaced across the call, are far more ambitious: legacy, world-stage recognition, a new consulting/speaking revenue line, multiple online revenue streams (video critique, weekly paid seminars), and global market expansion. He frames a growth ambition in the language of contentment. **PM read: do not take "just cover costs" at face value as the success bar; it is a low-risk emotional framing masking a legacy-and-reach ambition.** A second smaller dissonance: he says "I'm not really chasing numbers... I'm more chasing quality," yet his entire reason for engaging is wider distribution and reach.

## 4. Expected Outcomes & Success Criteria

**What he pictured when signing up.** A way to get his proven method "out there" to a global audience, with AI acting as a credible extension of himself, plus new revenue lines (consulting, speaking, coach education, parent education, and video-critique).
> "I'd like to basically show the world, if I could... how to train a player."
> "it's also to coaches... coaches who want to learn... I would like to pursue that market too... parents too."

**What specific result makes it a win.** His explicitly stated minimum bar is cost recovery, which Lucas affirmed as easily achievable:
> Ashley: "if I can cover costs with you, honestly speaking... I'm fine with that."
> Lucas: "I would be shocked with your track record... We wouldn't be able to cover the costs at a minimum within the first couple months. I think that'd be very, very doable."

Beyond that floor, the real win signals are reach and new opportunity: more speaking/consulting invitations, his framework being seen, and the AI behaving like his actual coaching judgment (right information, right timing, player-specific).

**Timeline expectation vs. our delivery timeline.** Ashley set no timeline of his own; he is patient and low-urgency ("I think I've got another 10 years"). Lucas set the pace, and Ashley accepted it without pushback:
> Lucas: "Within about 14 to 18 days, we would want to have a version 1.0 of both of those things."
> Lucas: "today's Monday, probably by Wednesday... we can share with you a preview of both the marketing... and... 40% of the way done with the AI brain."
> Ashley: "Sounds good." / "Sounds great."

**Gap to watch:** Lucas committed to a Wednesday preview and a 14-18 day v1.0. Ashley's own urgency is near zero, which is a stall risk (see Section 6): the vendor is driving timeline, not the client.

## 5. Product Insights for PM

**Feature gaps / requests (implied or stated).**
- **Video-critique feature (client-originated request).** He explicitly raised AI-assisted analysis of player-submitted match/practice video as a revenue stream: "send me, you know, a video of them playing, and then you give critique... to be able to do that on this platform would be good... one of the revenue streams." This is a concrete feature ask, not just chatter, and worth flagging to product even if v1 is text-only.
- **Live/recurring monetization surfaces.** "online chats or online forum or a seminar once a week and give a seminar question and answer once a week, sign in for, you know, however much." Suggests demand for community + recurring paid live events as upsell tiers.
- **Timing/sequencing intelligence in the brain.** His differentiator is *when* and *to whom* knowledge is delivered, not the raw content: "the information might be given to you, but is it in the correct timing... in that player-specific development?" The brain should encode his pyramid sequencing and player-stage logic, not just dump facts.
- **Three-question character qualifier (great structured input).** He already has a hard qualification gate: "do you have a great attitude? Are you coachable?... do you have a strong work ethic?" This maps directly to a qualification/segmentation flow in the AI funnel.

**Onboarding friction points.**
- **Tool literacy gap.** "What is Slack?" — resolved by switching to WhatsApp. Low friction here because the team adapted, but it signals he needs the lightest-touch, most familiar tooling possible; avoid introducing new platforms.
- **Open IP-handling question he could not resolve confidently.** "Should I take this stuff off the web... or keep it there?" He answered it himself with an Elon Musk quote, but PM should give a clear, confident recommendation so this doesn't resurface as hesitation.
- **No clear single source of truth handed over yet.** He offered the book PDF, website material, and presentations but nothing was transferred on the call; momentum depends on him sending assets via WhatsApp.

**Trust / confidence signals (relevant to Trust Report and Brain Health dashboard).**
- He is highly sensitive to credibility and source authority — he judges competitors by experience: "what is your experience, and where are you getting this information from?... It might be just a young guy who's done it for five or ten years." **The Trust Report should foreground source provenance (his book, his 40 years, named mentors) so the AI's answers feel experience-backed, not generic.**
- He worries the AI will lose his nuance (timing, player-specificity). Brain Health surfacing *coverage of his frameworks* and flagging gaps would directly address this. He even volunteered to record one-on-one video to fill gaps: "I could do a little bit more one-on-one... video of certain questions or certain topics." This is a ready-made remediation path the Brain Health dashboard can trigger.

**Stall risks.**
- **Low personal urgency.** "I think I've got another 10 years." No deadline pressure of his own.
- **"Just cover costs" framing** sets an emotionally safe, low bar that could let him disengage if early results feel modest — even though his real ambitions are larger.
- **Asset-delivery dependency.** Launch needs his book PDF and materials; he is busy on-court 6 hours/day in summer ("we're doing seven to two"), so handoff could lag.
- **Quality-over-volume identity** may make him ambivalent about aggressive marketing if it ever feels like "chasing numbers."

**Messaging / positioning language to mirror in marketing (his own words).**
- "Pocket coach" framing (Lucas's term, but it fits his model). Use AI-as-extension-of-the-coach.
- **"How to train a player from character"** and the **"nine-stage player performance pyramid"** — his signature IP; lead with it.
- **"My goal is to make you good, but it's not to make you comfortable."** Strong, ownable brand line.
- **"We don't take everybody"** + the three character questions (attitude, coachable, work ethic) — exclusivity and qualification positioning.
- **"Hunger and desire," "tenacity and resilience"** — his top character qualities, usable in copy.
- **Accessibility / anti-elitism angle:** "I want to make it kind of more accessible to people all over the world rather than just the US who are super affluent." This justifies the low-ticket entry offer and the global frame.
- **Authority proof points:** "I send hundreds and hundreds of people to college... it's in the millions of dollars now"; "two players, one in the world"; trained under "three out of four" of the best coaches in the world.

## 6. Stall Risk Score

**Score: 3 / 5 (moderate).**

He is engaged, identity-aligned with innovation, agreeable to the timeline, and even generates feature/revenue ideas himself, which all reduce risk; but his near-zero personal urgency ("I think I've got another 10 years"), his deliberately low "just cover costs" success bar, and a launch dependency on him sending assets while working six-hour on-court days create real follow-through risk. The danger is not rejection but quiet drift, where he stays warm and friendly yet slow to deliver materials and slow to push the offer live.

**Recommended intervention:** In the WhatsApp channel, immediately convert his enthusiasm into one tiny, dated action — request the book PDF and the website presentation files within 24 hours — and tie the Wednesday preview explicitly to a reframed, ambition-sized win (new consulting/speaking leads and global reach), not just "covering costs," so the success bar matches his real underlying job and he stays pulled rather than passive.
