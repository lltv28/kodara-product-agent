## 1. The Switch Event

**What was happening right before he reached out.** Dillon was deep in a self-built systems sprint and had stopped selling, which directly threatened his revenue. He says it plainly: "May has been really [bad]. I've done no selling because I've been building the systems" and "I can't afford to not have a good month... **The trigger / how he found us.** A cold ad, not a referral: "the ad came up on Instagram. It was like, all right, let me give this a go." The pitch itself (from Noor) was the conversion event: "Noor has delivered an epic pitch... everything about the way you showed up, the way it was pitched, everything about it was right. Which is why we dove in."

**The breaking points (two distinct ones):**
1. **Channel dependency decay.** His partner-driven lead flow is drying up. Conversion on ticket sales has fallen from a peak of "50% conversion" down to "20% conversions on ticket sales," and "I don't think the partners are doing quite as much to bring new leads in. So naturally, numbers are falling. So we now need to decouple from that dependency and build our own lead source."
2. **The one-man-band ceiling.** "I'm a one-man band" / "me and my Claude and my ChatGPT, that's all there is." He personally writes all sequences, does WhatsApp closing by hand, and delivers every live event, so growth is capped by his hours.

**What he tried before and why it fell short.**
- **OpenClaw agent (DIY):** "I kind of used it when I was building out an OpenClaw agent... And then, of course, Claude smashed it with Cowork and Perplexity launched computer. So OpenClaw just is now sitting dead in my study, not doing anything." The DIY build was made obsolete by platform shifts before it shipped.
- **ChatGPT-as-closer (manual):** He copy-pastes both ways by hand: "it's ChatGPT writing the responses for me... I'll keep plugging those messages from Neil back into ChatGPT." Works, but is entirely manual and non-scalable.
- **Pricing/offer experiments that failed:** Tried "£18,000, £15,000 plus VAT" six-week program ("converting, but not at volume... everything was like little"); tried a 3-tier follow-up offer ("didn't work because I think there were too many choices"); tried "£16,000 and a £20,000" tier — "that just didn't sell."
- **Obsidian / AI second brain:** Built one, but found it inert without distribution: "I have an AI second brain... But what use is that if it's not plugged into the Backbone, the Ascension Engine?"

## 3. Functional, Emotional, and Social Jobs

**Functional job.** Build an AI-powered system that (a) generates and customizes content to a segmented 10,000-person list, (b) drives conversions through the existing ladder, and (c) raises client lifetime value — without him personally doing it. In his words: "customized content for like every single lead... Customization was critical, and then conversion... wanted the AI to drive to conversion... And so the third thing was client lifetime value." Concretely, the headline functional deliverable he keeps returning to is the demand-analysis tool: "Who are the providers in my area and what do they want? What's currently on the market that will meet that demand and how much money will I make?... How do I create a pitch to go and raise investor finance." Plus an AI avatar to produce social content he refuses to film himself.

**Emotional job.** He wants to feel **safe about hitting his number** and **relieved of the build-vs-sell trap.** "all of this is perfectly timed... I can't afford to not have a good month." He also wants the **confidence of betting on what already works** rather than gambling: "let's scale what really works rather than going through this process of trying to reiterate and create something new. And then we realise that actually there's this bit of magic that's missing." There is also pride/validation: he repeatedly praises the pitch ("you deserve my credit card number for that pitch... You guys have nailed it") — he wants to feel he picked a peer who operates at his level.

**Social job.** He wants to be perceived as a **credible, established authority even to cold audiences**, closing the gap between his real reputation and his thin social footprint: "if they see me on an ad... They're going to go to my profile. They're going to see I've got [no] content, a thousand followers... yeah, something doesn't ring true there." He also wants to protect his identity as **"an investor first... not an influencer... not here just to sell you courses"** and as a mission-driven leader ("we want to house 10,000 vulnerable people by 2030"). The Tony Robbins aspiration is explicitly social/status: "Ultimately, I want, like, a hologram in front of the room... convert 20% to a [£2k] product, like Tony Robbins style."

**Cognitive dissonance flags.**
- **"Scale what works" vs. constant offer-churning.** He preaches stability ("if it works, kind of let's scale what really works") yet his own history is a string of restructures (£18k, 3-tier, £16k/£20k all tried and dropped). The stated driver is discipline; the underlying driver looks like searching for an offer that finally scales.
- **"AI can do exactly what I'm doing" vs. "no one can replace me."** Within minutes: "I'm very aware that the AI can do exactly what I'm doing" and "no one can replace me on the one day." The stated belief is openness to automation; the underlying attachment is to his own irreplaceability on the live stage. This is the central tension to manage.
- **"I'm open to everything" vs. "I've tested something and this is where we're at."** He positions as flexible but is anchored to past test results, which can read as resistance to new recommendations (e.g., he immediately pushed back on Lucas's price-increase suggestion).
