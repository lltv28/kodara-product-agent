# Marva Allen (Wordeee) — Onboarding Call JTBD Analysis — 2026-05-26

**Client:** Marva Allen, founder of Wordeee, a seven-year-old hybrid media and publishing company (Wordy TV, Wordy University, and book publishing). In this transcript her dialogue is labeled "Hue-Man Bookstore."
**Call type:** Kodara onboarding call (post-sale, with Lucas Lee-Tyson and team member Ricardo Carmona; sale set up by Noor).
**Kodara scope discussed:** $3,500/month engagement to build an AI qualification/funnel product plus a marketing engine that drives traffic to a $17 / $50 entry offer and ultimately the $7,500 publishing package.

---

## 1. The Switch Event

**What was happening right before they reached out.** Wordeee had spent seven years building product and delivering white-glove service while deliberately ignoring marketing. Marva: *"we've been heads down building our system and delivering our services. Now we're ready to buy the hype"* (lines 602-603). All growth to date came from referrals: *"we have never marketed ourselves. Everything has always been word of mouth"* (line 75).

**The breaking point / moment of frustration.** Two pressures converged. First, market saturation from low-quality entrants: *"with the deluge of people who are trying to enter the market, probably all for the wrong reasons, I thought we needed a louder voice. So we needed to reach more people. We needed people to know who we were"* (lines 79-81). Second, a funding/competitive deadline: *"we're out at the investment marketplace, so we need to show first mover advantage"* (line 659).

**The literal trigger to buy.** A Facebook ad. *"whatever brought the Facebook release... I saw you... I said, oh, this funnel thing, this is what we really need to at least test the market to see how we can get traffic to ourself... It was a low enough cost investment for us to do it"* (lines 650-656). She also frames it almost fatalistically: *"I'm putting my trust in faith in you because somehow the universe said I should"* (line 649).

**What they tried before and why it fell short.** Word of mouth was their only channel for seven years and, while it sustained them, it could not scale awareness: *"those who matter know who we are, but we needed to somehow kind of light a little Kindle that would get our name... propagated far better"* (lines 82-83). They had already built their own internal AI (an editorial manuscript reader) but explicitly *not* a marketing capability: *"AI, we've just built our own AI products, in terms of editorial stuff... Not marketing stuff"* (lines 161-163). The marketing automation arm is something they intend to build only *"as soon as we're funded"* (line 301), so Kodara is filling a gap they cannot yet staff internally.

## 3. The Functional, Emotional, and Social Jobs

**Functional job.** Build a marketing/funnel engine plus an AI qualifier that drives strangers into Wordeee's ladder ($17/$50 entry to the $7,500 publishing package) and proves the channel works. In her words: *"this funnel thing... is what we really need to at least test the market to see how we can get traffic to ourself"* (line 654), and the explicit revenue target: *"we want to make our $3,500 back in $17 signups"* (line 609). Success metrics named: collect opt-in email names, increase site traffic (lines 605-606).

**Emotional job.** To finally feel ready and safe to be visible after years of deliberate quiet, and to feel reassured rather than exposed. *"We really haven't bought a lot of the hype because we've been heads down... Now we're ready to buy the hype... We're at that place where we can"* (lines 602-604). The reassurance need shows in the agreement and IP-protection moments (lines 13-17, 538-543). There is also a hopeful, almost surrendering optimism: *"I'm putting my trust in faith in you because somehow the universe said I should"* (line 649).

**Social job.** To be perceived as the legitimate, prestige, ethical alternative to exploitative traditional publishers, and to be visible to the right people, including investors. She wants Wordeee recognized: *"We needed people to know who we were"* (line 81), to *"become one of the top SEO people that go to"* (line 84), and to *"show first mover advantage"* to the investment marketplace (line 659). Her positioning identity is explicit: *"we're creating the middle-class writer"* (lines 455-457).

**Cognitive dissonance flag.** Her stated job is marketing/lead generation, but a strong underlying driver is **fundraising/valuation signaling**: *"we're out at the investment marketplace, so we need to show first mover advantage"* (line 659) and *"as soon as we're funded, we'll be working on an automated marketing arm"* (line 301). The funnel may be valued by her less as a profit center and more as proof of traction for investors, which is a different success bar than the $17-signup ROI she also cites (line 609). Second tension: she sells a premium, *"all custom... white glove service"* identity (lines 69-71) yet is buying a productized, AI-driven, paid-acquisition funnel; she partly resolves this by walling the AI off from her *"proprietary system"* (line 538), keeping the bespoke core sacred while letting Kodara handle only top-of-funnel.

## 4. Expected Outcomes & Success Criteria

**What she pictured at signup.** A low-cost market test of paid traffic: *"this is what we really need to at least test the market to see how we can get traffic to ourself"* (line 654); *"this is our marketing budget that we are allocating now, and we've not done it before. So we want to see how it works"* (lines 610-611).

**Specific results that = a win.** Her own stated success criteria (lines 605-609):
- Collect opt-in names / email signups: *"we would like to be able to have to collect names... people opt into our email"* (line 605).
- Measurable traffic lift: *"We'd like to be able to see the traffic increase on our site"* (line 606), which she tracks via Mandrill/analytics (line 607).
- Direct ROI: *"we want to make our $3,500 back in $17 signups"* (line 609).
- Implicit higher bar: enough demonstrable traction to support the investment raise / first-mover claim (line 659).

**Timeline expectation vs. Kodara delivery.** She expects a checkpoint before continuing: *"I get to see the first product to decide or something"* (line 474), tied to the $3,500/month and the first-month refund guarantee Lucas restates (lines 506-508). Lucas set delivery at *"14 to 18 days"* for v1.0 of both the AI and the marketing assets, with *"within that first month... you've pretty much seen everything"* (lines 487, 497-501). No conflict surfaced on timeline; the dependency risk is on **her** side: delivery speed is gated on *"if we get feedback quickly from you"* (line 497) and on her sending source materials (FAQs, flowcharts, samples, the platform walkthrough video, a deck — lines 515-542).

## 5. Product Insights for PM

**Feature gaps / requests implied.**
- **AI manuscript-qualifier that mirrors her existing internal tool.** She wants the funnel AI to read/assess a manuscript and route the author to "ready → platform" vs. "not ready → Wordy University" or a live class. Supporting: *"it can say, yes, you're ready... or no, you're not ready, go to Word[eee] University"* (lines 363-365); *"Yes, exactly"* (line 357). Note constraint: she does **not** want our AI fed her proprietary editorial system (line 538), so the qualifier must be a lighter, independent assessment, not a clone of her internal reader.
- **Multi-tier offer ladder in the funnel, not a single CTA.** Entry points already exist ($17 feedback, $50 manuscript upload, $249.95 live class with discount, $7,500 package) and she wants the funnel to route by readiness and budget. Supporting: *"some people, maybe they don't have the finances, or they don't have the finished manuscript... figuring out kind of a library of options"* (lines 482-483); discounted live class as a fallback step (lines 477-478).
- **Reporting/attribution dashboard** that reconciles with her own stack (Mandrill, Norris). Supporting: *"we record all of this stuff with Mandrill and Norris"* (line 607); Lucas promised week-over-week / month-over-month reports (lines 614-617).
- **Potential future channel partnership / reseller angle:** she floats offering Kodara-style marketing to her own authors (coaches, speakers). Supporting: *"if they're coaches or if they're whatever, this is kind of a... great thing for them to be able to say, go work with these guys"* (lines 645-646). PM should flag as expansion/referral opportunity, not v1 scope.

**Onboarding friction points.**
- **Missing agreement at kickoff** — first thing she raised, twice: *"Noor said... I was going to get some documents... but I didn't get anything"* (lines 13-14); *"the agreement that states all the terms and conditions"* (line 17). Trust-eroding gap in handoff from sales (Noor) to delivery.
- **No Slack / collaboration tool in place** — onboarding requires her technical team to stand up Slack: *"I can have our technical team set up Slack"* (line 582). Adds a dependency and a day or two of latency before the shared channel exists.
- **Repeated need to clarify IP boundaries** — she circled the "don't feed AI our proprietary system" concern multiple times (lines 536-545), indicating it must be addressed explicitly and in writing.
- **Connection-quality issues** early in the call (lines 21-46) ate time; minor, but the substantive discovery started late.

**Trust and confidence signals (relevant to Trust Report / Brain Health dashboard).**
- IP isolation is a hard trust requirement; Lucas's reassurance — *"we don't train any of our models on client files... all of it is strictly in your account"* (lines 556-557) — is exactly the message a Trust Report should make visible and verifiable to her.
- She wants to "mark it only for internal readers" (line 545); the Brain Health dashboard should surface which uploaded assets are AI-ingested vs. internal-only, so she can self-verify the boundary.
- She is technical (25 years in technology, line 85) and will scrutinize claims, so transparency artifacts will land well with her.

**Stall risks.**
- **Delivery is gated on her inputs.** Lucas: v1 speed depends on *"if we get feedback quickly from you"* (line 497) and on her sending FAQs, flowcharts, samples, the platform video, and a deck (lines 515-542). She is busy, runs a high-touch ops cadence (*"They call it all hours of the day and night,"* line 211), and her team is in Lebanon (line 164) — material handoff and feedback could slip.

- **Funding dependency.** Her broader marketing ambitions are *"as soon as we're funded"* (line 301); if the raise stalls or shifts priorities, attention to this engagement could go quiet.
- **Shifting/ambiguous success bar** between hard ROI ($3,500 back in $17 signups, line 609) and softer investor-signaling (line 659) makes "success" harder to definitively prove, raising the chance she feels it "isn't working."

**Messaging / positioning language to mirror in marketing.** Her own phrases are strong, on-brand copy:

After your friends and family have bought it"* (lines 416-419).
- The "tribe" concept: *"it's your fans that's going to make you really successful... To create a tribe, what we call a tribe"* (lines 196, 232).
- Proof point worth featuring (with permission): a *"nobody author"* whose ~$3,500 investment yielded *"$50,000 or $60,000 on the back end"* (lines 522-523), and the Jay Manuel / Netflix documentary story (lines 200-203).

## 6. Stall Risk Score

**Score: 3 / 5 (moderate).**

She is genuinely motivated and already paid, with clear push (no marketing for seven years, market saturation, an investor deadline) and concrete success metrics, which pulls risk down; but delivery is dependent on her supplying source materials and fast feedback while she runs a high-touch operation with an overseas team, there is a natural "see the first product to decide" checkpoint plus a first-month refund, and her success bar drifts between hard ROI and softer investor signaling, all of which push risk up. **Recommended intervention:** immediately send the missing signed agreement and a single short "asset request" checklist (FAQs, flowcharts, samples, platform walkthrough video, deck — all flagged internal-only per her IP concern), with a named date for the v1 review, so the engagement does not stall waiting on her inputs or on lingering trust gaps.
