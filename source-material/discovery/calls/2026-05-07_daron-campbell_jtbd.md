## 1. The Switch Event

The trigger is not a crisis but a "perfect storm" of opportunity colliding with a personal readiness moment. Daron has 34 years and "$4.5 billion worth of transactions" of expertise and now sees the market conditions and his own life timing as the moment to scale a coaching brand nationally.

- **What was happening right before he reached out:** He is actively trying to scale and "blow this thing up." He is shopping aggressively for marketing solutions: "I look at and listen to 20 pitches or 20 marketing concepts a week, literally, because I'm trying to figure out how to blow this thing up." Simultaneously he is building the capital side of the business in Houston ("I started a private lending company to piggyback on our real estate company... I'm here for three days to meet with all these private lenders who want to place money").
- **The readiness/breaking-point moment:** A felt sense that now is the time: "now is that perfect storm in my mind to teach people... it's just time for me to take this broad and to scale it and for me to become the real estate... real estate for the rest of us." The constraint he names is reach, not knowledge: "I just got to get out there. I have to be known. And, you know, I'm known in L.A., and that's about it. So we got to go beyond."
- **What he tried before and why it fell short:**
  - **Self-run Meta ads + a free mini course:** "I paid Meta $100 a day for 60 days. We sold 72 mentorships. We got 2,000 people to download a free mini course." This worked but left money on the table.
  - **No financing partner:** "if I had fan bases or WAP or some kind of financing, I'd have probably sold 400 people. No joke... I've never been approved with the financing sources." A clear unmet gap.
  - **No nurture/drip system:** "what I haven't done well is... dripped on those people. If that guy had seen me every week for the last year, he probably would have signed up six months ago." He admits one-off email blasts converted dormant leads, but he never built the engine.
  - **ChatGPT brainstorming as a stopgap:** "last night, me and my chat GPT went through a bunch of things... My chat GPT and I brainstormed a bunch of things to figure out... what's some good bait to get people really in." He is trying to DIY the front end and recognizes he needs help.
  - **Self-described skill gap:** "I don't have the audience and I'm a non-techie techie. So that's why you guys make sense to me."

How he found Kodara: Alex Hormozi-style paid ads. "that's how I found you guys. I mean, they work."

## 3. The Functional, Emotional, and Social Jobs

- **Functional job:** Build a marketing + AI engine that generates reach and a low-ticket front-end offer that pulls strangers into his ecosystem and converts them to his $5,000 mentorship at scale. In his words: "What is the automated part that we want everybody to want? That's that $17 item." And: "what I need is the marketing engine that keeps us top of mind." The concrete spec he keeps returning to is a $17 product that delivers Daron's acquisition formula plus a real-time list of distressed deals in the buyer's own zip codes: "it would be golden if our automated $17 whatever gave them the formula and an example in their neighborhood."
- **Emotional job:** To feel that his life's expertise finally gets the audience it deserves and that he is no longer the bottleneck. He wants the relief and excitement of momentum: "I'm excited, man... I believe it with every bone in my body." He wants to feel he can "never run out of stories to tell" and that the business has "legs" and "longevity."
- **Social job:** To be recognized as a top-tier guru — peer of Pace Morby, Grant Cardone, and Alex Hormozi — with a "lifetime raving fans" following. "for me to become the real estate... I call it real estate for the rest of us." And: "with him and Pace Morby... like Alex Hormozi, they got lifetime raving fans. And that's what I want to build. People who just follow the Daron Campbell brand." He explicitly wants to surpass them: "what we can do can surpass all of what we've seen these gurus do."

**Cognitive dissonance flags:**
1. **Stated front-end goal vs. underlying belief.** He says he loves the low-ticket "way" ("Your way is so much better"), but his ingrained belief is high-ticket-first ("I believe in high ticket... you can't give it away"). His instinct to over-deliver on the $17 ("I don't have a problem in the $17 product to give... the formula... and 10 properties in Orange County") collides with Lucas's caution about overwhelm. The driver underneath the "low ticket" enthusiasm is really lead-gen volume for the $5k, not the front-end product itself.
2. **"Foolproof"/volume confidence vs. his own conversion history.** He says "you and I are going to sell 50 of them a day" and "I'd have sold hundreds," yet his actual track record is 72 mentorships from 2,000 downloads over 90 days. The stated expectation outruns the demonstrated math.
3. **"Different/blue ocean" positioning vs. heavy guru mimicry.** He wants to be "different, a different flavor," but frames the whole vision as borrowing Morby's sub-two, Cardone's apartment fund, and Shelby Sapp's certified-finder model. The underlying driver is status parity with those names more than genuine category differentiation.

## 5. Product Insights for PM

- **Feature gap — geo/real-time deal-list generation inside the AI product.** This is the most concrete, repeated feature ask and the heart of his $17 offer concept. "if our AI has the capacity to give a custom something to each person... Here's Daron's creative method. Here's the formula. And Lucas, you're in Orange County. Here's 20 deals within an hour drive of you." He believes the data is public/scrapeable: "That data is public information even... notices of default, notices of trustee sales, foreclosures, bankruptcies, that's all public information." **PM decision:** validate whether the AI/product can ingest or generate live notice-of-default data by zip code, or whether this must be scoped to static examples. He currently spends "$10 grand a month" buying this from "eight different sources," so accuracy and freshness expectations are high. If we cannot do live data, set that expectation explicitly before he over-promises it in the $17 SKU.
- **Feature/integration need — consumer financing (WAP/Affirm).** A named, quantified conversion lever: "if I had fan bases or WAP... I'd have probably sold 400 people." Lucas committed: "We have a pretty good relationship, mainly with WAP." **PM decision:** prioritize the financing integration into the $5k mentorship checkout; it is a stated win condition, not a nice-to-have.
- **Onboarding friction — front-end product scope is unresolved and is the client's open question.** He explicitly hands it to us: "solidifying the give, the automated part... that's where I'm going to lean on you guys to help. Let's crystallize that. What is the automated part that we want everybody to want?" There is unresolved tension between his urge to over-deliver and Lucas's overwhelm caution ("very easy for people to get overwhelmed... a confused mind does" nothing). **PM decision:** the first deliverable (the plan) must lock the single "one thing" of the $17 offer; leaving it open is the top friction point.
- **Onboarding friction — none on logistics; client is highly cooperative.** He uses Slack ("Yeah, absolutely"), has abundant assets ready ("VSLs... ads... scissor reel... podcasts... eBooks... before and after pictures... 500 videos"), and signals fast response. The friction risk is asset overload, not asset scarcity: "I got so much stuff in the can that... a bunch of stuff you guys won't want to use." **PM decision:** give him a tight, prioritized intake list (course + calls + ebooks + case-study videos first) so the brain build is not slowed by triage.

Watch for claims that need compliance review (income/earnings promises like "make at least $50,000 on that deal, or I'll coach you until you do" and "kind of foolproof").
- **Stall risk signals.** Primary stall vector is not the client going quiet (he is the opposite) but: (a) the conversion math gap — his "50 a day"/"400 people" expectation vs. the 72/2,000 actual — which could curdle into disappointment if early results are normal; and (b) scope sprawl — he repeatedly floats adjacent verticals (private lending, "trained money brokers," Cardone-style apartment syndication fund) that could divert focus from launching tier one. ## 6. Stall Risk Score

**Score: 2 / 5 (low stall risk).**

This client is exceptionally engaged, asset-rich, financially committed, cooperative on tooling, and explicitly biased toward moving faster than the team ("I want to go faster than... you won't have any problem with me on response times"), which all point to a fast launch. The residual risk that keeps it off a 1 is expectation mismatch (his "sell 50 a day" / "400 people" confidence against a 72-from-2,000 actual track record) and scope sprawl into adjacent verticals, either of which could stall momentum after launch if early numbers feel ordinary.

**Recommended intervention:** In the first marketing-plan deliverable, set explicit, evidence-based conversion benchmarks (anchored to his real 72/2,000 history with a clear improvement path via nurture + financing), lock the single "one thing" of the $17 offer, and formally park the other verticals as Phase 2+ so tier-one launch stays the sole focus.
