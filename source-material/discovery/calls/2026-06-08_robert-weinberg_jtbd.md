## 1. The Switch Event

**What was happening in the business right before he reached out.**
Robert is a 20-plus-year, one-man-show mortgage broker whose organic content engine has started working, but he has no way to monetize the people it attracts before they're ready to transact. A YouTube video on DSCR loans created a flood of high-intent leads, most of whom are not ready now.

- "But for me, the big change is I've been in the mortgage business for 20-plus years, and it's a high-ticket sale... it's also a really long sales cycle."
- "I had a video or two go viral on YouTube, which had people reaching out to me to do mortgages... like one video on YouTube, I think I've got like 20 mortgage deals, closed deals, like done. But I have so many people calling me that aren't ready right now. They're three months, six months, a year away."

**The breaking point / moment of frustration.**
The manual follow-up loop on not-ready leads is the recurring pain. He calls it a "hamster wheel" twice, and he is doing it all himself.

- "So I end up in this follow-up loop where I'm every three... It's not an AI follow-up. It's me personally following up. And, you know, it's this hamster wheel, right?"
- "95% of those people will never even do a loan. I need to capture that 95% with something on a continuity. For an upfront AI or whatever it might be."
- "I've hated that about the mortgage business because there's no real front-end product."

**What he had tried before and why it fell short.**
He has built every adjacent authority asset, and none of them monetize the front end or do the nurturing he needs.

- Book: "I did a book years ago, I do have a book on Amazon... It definitely establishes me as an authority... but I feel like they aren't doing what you're saying the AI is doing, which is nurturing and really giving people the knowledge and info."
- Radio show and podcast: "I've done a radio show for years. I have a podcast. So I've done all these things, but..." (same shortfall as the book).
- A paid course: "I tried selling a course years ago for $599. I spent months getting the course ready, and I only had one person buy it at the end."
- Financial-planning front end (the path other LOs take): "I don't have the licensing for that, and I really don't [know] that much about it."
- Existing AI tools used only for personal efficiency, not as a product: "I'm using AI every day in my business already... ChatGPT, Claude. I'm just using it to be more efficient. I haven't gotten to the level of doing, like, Claude code and co-work and all that, but I'm getting the itch."

## 3. The Functional, Emotional, and Social Jobs

**Functional job (the practical task).**
Insert a low-ticket, automated product between his free YouTube/Google leads and his calendar so the 95% who aren't ready now can be monetized, educated, qualified, and nurtured without his manual time, and routed to a call when they are ready.

- "I need something in between." / "this would more just be kind of like a moat between the video and then booking with me."
- Tier vision: cheap diagnostic, then a recurring "pocket advisor" that scouts and underwrites deals: "I'm interested in single-family homes in Akron, Ohio... then the AI goes out, pulls all the listings... and then would run the DSCR numbers... Here's the top five deals that have the highest DSCR ratio."

**Emotional job (how he wants to feel).**
Relieved of the hamster wheel and the part of the work he dislikes; hopeful and validated that a passive, automated income stream is achievable.

- "honestly, I don't even like doing mortgages. If I can do enough here, like I just do the low ticket AI product."
- "you've given me hope in this conversation that that actually could be possible and feasible."
- "I'm excited just talking about it."

**Social job (how he wants to be perceived).**
As the recognized expert/pioneer of consumer mortgage AI, who graduates from grinding broker to "stage expert" and category leader his peer community looks to.

- "I'm the only person that's branded my name as a person."
- "be the stage expert, and then saying, hey, I'm going to have Joe from my team take care of you."
- To his 135-member school.com mortgage community: "I'm not even selling mortgages anymore. I made 78 grand last month selling this AI product. You can do the same." / "nobody... has talked about creating an AI product for consumers."

**Cognitive dissonance flag.**
His stated reason for the project is monetizing the not-ready 95% (a front-end nurture tool), but his underlying driver is an exit from mortgage origination altogether and a new identity as an AI-product entrepreneur. He says "honestly, I don't even like doing mortgages" and "I would like to not be in the mortgage business anymore and be in your business," and sets a hard income target ("I'd need to make around $500,000... in net income"). The PM risk: he frames Kodara as a lead-nurture moat, but he will measure success as a standalone $500K revenue line and a path out of his core business. There is also a second dissonance: he prizes his personal phone follow-up as his highest converter ("my highest conversion generally is getting on the phone with a client") while asking the AI to absorb exactly that follow-up work he is attached to.

## 4. Expected Outcomes & Success Criteria

**What he pictured when signing up.**
- Organic, hands-off sales of the AI product: "what really excited me was getting organic, like people actually go by the AI without me having to, like, find those people."
- A two-path entry (investor vs. first-time buyer) that qualifies then routes: "the first couple of questions would be, which are you in? Are you a real estate investor? Or are you a first-time homebuyer?"
- A recurring deal-scouting/underwriting advisor at scale: "go in my AI, pay the $89 a month, and every month go in there multiple times and have it tell you the hottest markets in the US for DSCR and the top properties for DSCR. And that alone would be enough for people to be paying every month like wildfire."
- An ownership/asset he might one day buy out or run on royalty: "are you guys flexible with us saying, hey, let's do like a royalty where you can just get 20% of every sale."

**What specific result makes this a win.**
A six-figure secondary income within 12 months, ideally scaling toward $500K net so he can step back from origination.

- "would you say a 12-month commitment should be reasonable enough to make, at least prove it, prove the concept and have some sort of six-figure, another secondary income from this product."
- "I'd need to make around $500,000 in... net income... then I'd be able to basically step back from my day to day, live the life of my dreams."

**Timeline expectation vs. actual delivery.**
- His proof horizon is patient: a full 12-month commitment, "go balls to the wall," not expecting overnight success. "If everything looks good, I plan to just pay for one year in full and just go balls to the wall and just see what happens." / "I'm not looking for an overnight success."
- Kodara's stated delivery: preview in 2-3 days ("by Wednesday"), v1.0 in 14-18 days, then fast iteration. "we can get things done usually for a version 1.0 within like 14 to 18 days." / "today's Monday, my goal would be by Wednesday, we could share with you a preview."
- Alignment: his long proof window comfortably exceeds the build timeline, so timeline mismatch risk is low; the gap to manage is between his patient mindset and his eventual $500K expectation.

## 5. Product Insights for PM

**Feature gaps or requests (implied or stated).**
- Live MLS/listing ingestion plus automated DSCR qualification per property is the headline feature he expects to drive recurring revenue. "the AI goes out, pulls all the listings of homes in Akron, Ohio, and then would run the DSCR numbers for them... Here's the top five deals that have the highest DSCR ratio." He differentiates it from generic tools: "we're actually going to turn that data into, will this property qualify for a DSCR loan? Tell you yes or no based on the data."
- Two-branch intake flow (investor vs. first-time homebuyer) with different state-eligibility logic (42 states DSCR vs. ~3-4 states first-time). "if they're a real estate investor, then... they go down one way... if they're a first-time homebuyer... I can only work in like maybe three or four states at most."
- KEAP CRM integration to learn his follow-up style and analyze 2,000+ past clients. "we can integrate with that... can analyze, review everyone that you already have in there." (His CRM: "K-E-A-P.")
- Calendar/booking handoff at the moment of intent: "then it can put them in my calendar."
- A mechanism to keep the brain current with changing guidelines/regulation (DSCR rules could change "by every day").
- Tiered monthly pricing structure (e.g., 10 evaluations vs. unlimited): discussed as "maybe $150 gets you 10 property evaluations and $500 is unlimited."

**Onboarding friction points.**
- He had to re-ask basic commercial-model questions that the earlier rep (Nor) left fuzzy: ownership vs. rental, annual vs. one-time, buyout/royalty. "I expected it was going to be like 50K and that's it and you own it." This is a sales-to-onboarding handoff gap.
- Confusion over scope of the "done-with-you" Facebook ads service: he assumed Kodara would set up audiences and the technical side, but learned it's a "click here, click here" handoff. "I thought he was going in, figuring out the audiences and how much and all that."
- Repeated need to clarify the low-ticket vs. monthly price points (asked at the end what others sell for), suggesting pricing wasn't clearly framed pre-call.

**Trust and confidence signals (relevant to Trust Report / Brain Health dashboard).**
- He cares deeply about accuracy and saying things the way he would: "if there's anything off that you notice... I wouldn't say that or this is inaccurate, we can catch that early." A Trust Report that surfaces accuracy and on-brand voice will land well.
- He explicitly wants currency/freshness of regulatory content — a Brain Health "last updated / coverage" signal directly addresses his stated anxiety: "I want to make sure the AI is on the up and up with that."
- He flagged content curation: drop pre-2022 videos as stale. "I would just be looking at, like, the last 24, 36 months... they don't have to go back to 2019." Brain Health should let him see and prune source recency.
- Strong existing trust assets to mirror as proof: "almost 500 five-star reviews," "I have more reviews than any other loan officer."

**Stall risks.**
- He outsources ads and is unsure he'll execute them: "I don't know how much I'm going to be able to jump in and actually do the ads myself." If launch depends on him running paid traffic, it may stall — needs a VA/agency handoff baked in.
- His own discipline depends on retraining: "I just got to retrain my mind that that's what I'm going for" (selling the purchase vs. defaulting to a free call booking). His instinct to bypass the funnel for hot leads ("It wouldn't even have them go through the AI... just say, hey, go book a call") could undercut the paid front end if not designed deliberately.
- He depends on a third party (Fast Forward Media) to embed the CTA in scripts; coordination outside Kodara's control. "they can build that into the scripting."
- His emotional momentum is high right now ("I'm excited just talking about it"), but excitement-led buyers can cool; the patient 12-month framing partly offsets this.

**Messaging / positioning language to mirror in marketing.**
- "a moat between the video and then booking with me."
- "monetize before they've even taken a loan application."
- "here's a way to monetize your knowledge."
- The customer-facing CTA framing he proposed himself: "if you're a serious investor and you're ready right now to look at your numbers, call me. But if you're just seeing this and you're not really sure if it's for you, then go ahead and download my AI."
- Customer disbelief hooks usable in copy: "too good to be true. How come I don't know about this?" / "I had no idea I could do this. You're the first person that's ever shown me this."
- Buyer pain for the consumer product: bank rejection for no-job/weird-income investors ("Okay, we can't help you then. The DSCR loan gets rid of that.").

## 6. Stall Risk Score

**Score: 2 / 5 (low-to-moderate; likely to launch).**

Justification: Robert has rare launch fuel — a proven organic lead engine (YouTube + 500-review Google profile), public content the team can ingest immediately, a one-year paid-in-full commitment, and visible emotional buy-in, so the inputs for a fast v1.0 are already in place. The moderate drag comes from his dependence on outside parties for traffic (an ad agency he's unsure he'll drive, plus the Fast Forward team for CTA scripting) and his instinct to route hot leads straight to a call rather than through the paid funnel, which could thin the front-end product he's paying to validate.

**Recommended intervention:** In the first Slack week, lock down the traffic path concretely — confirm which VA or agency will run the "click here, click here" ads and get the Fast Forward team to commit to a specific dual CTA in upcoming video scripts ("serious investor → call; not sure → try the AI") — so launch never waits on an owner who has told us he won't run ads himself.

## Top 3 Takeaways for the PM Team (ranked)

1. **Build the funnel around the not-ready 95%, and protect it from his own hot-lead instinct.** The entire job he hired us for is monetizing leads who aren't ready to transact ("I need to capture that 95% with something on a continuity"), yet he floated bypassing the AI to send ready buyers straight to a call. Design the front-end so even hot leads pass through a paid diagnostic, and coach him on the "retrain my mind" point so the paid product actually gets a fair test.

2. **Prioritize the recurring deal-scouting/DSCR-qualification advisor — that is where he sees the real money and the moat.** His excitement, his pricing willingness ("Investors would pay that all day, hundreds a month"), and his differentiation claim ("we're actually going to turn that data into, will this property qualify for a DSCR loan? Tell you yes or no") all center on listing ingestion + automated underwriting. Scope this for the recurring tier early; it is also his stated path to the $500K exit goal.

3. **Close the commercial-model and ownership gaps left open from the sales handoff, and signal freshness via Brain Health.** He repeatedly probed rental vs. ownership, annual cost, buyout, and royalty ("Is it you guys own it and you're renting it to me?"), and he is anxious about regulatory content going stale ("on the up and up... it could change by every day"). A clear written model summary plus a Brain Health "source recency / last updated" view directly defuse his two named anxieties and reduce churn risk on the annual renewal.
