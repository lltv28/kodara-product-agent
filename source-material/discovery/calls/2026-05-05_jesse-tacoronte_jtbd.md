## 1. The Switch Event

Jesse is an established 58-year-old funding and credit-repair operator who already does over a million dollars a month in processing volume. He did not come to Kodara because his business was failing. The trigger was a moment of possibility recognition, not a breaking point: after his first conversations with Kodara's Noor, he realized AI could productize 20+ years of stored expertise in a way he could not execute himself.

- The literal switch moment: "when it hit me, I'm an OCD, ADHD, like, you know, it just hit. I I'm like, holy . I swear I walked through in the morning. I'm like, I could do this. I could do that... So I might have two or three operating systems with you" (lines 27-33). "I was up three in the day in the morning thinking about this" (line 12).
- What was happening right before: he was in active build/hire churn. "I was firing, hiring sales teams. I mean, was doing all kinds of stuff the last two weeks" (lines 23-24).
- The realization that he'd been doing things the hard way: he had a team member ("a girl, took her four months to transfer over") manually moving 20 years of content into Notion, and had another teammate re-dub a competitor's videos with his avatar, only to learn from Noor "oh, you didn't have to do that. We can do that... So I've been through all this freaking trouble for nothing, right?" (lines 73-74, 104-111).
- The deeper push driver is a decades-old regret about missing the last technology wave, surfaced through his late father-in-law Barry: "never, ever get behind on technology" and "if I had just put five or 10 grand into Google and forgot about it, dude... I'd be a billionaire" (lines 193-204, 195, 222).

**What he tried before and why it fell short:**
- DIY AI tools: "I went with Chad GPT, then I moved over to Claude, and now open Claude's company. And I'm, like, trying to stay with it, right? But I don't have the knowledge you do, man... I can't incorporate it the way I want it to. I can tell you what I want, but I can't. I don't have the engineers" (lines 205-216).
- His own Notion + basic AI build: "I have a whole system in the back end, which is notion. And within Notion, we already have AI working... It's not as complicated as yours, obviously" (lines 48-51).
- Manual labor workarounds (4-month content transfer, avatar re-dubbing) that turned out to be unnecessary with proper tooling (lines 73, 104-111).

## 3. The Functional, Emotional, and Social Jobs

**Functional job:** Turn 20+ years of stored expertise (Notion, school content, ~5,000 hours of calls, webinars, competitor course logins) into an AI-powered, productized offer ladder he can sell, $17 entry → $499 + $199/mo school → $7-10K done-for-you / VIP, plus marketing and AI spokesperson ads to drive it. "that's really all I want to get off my system... how do we incorporate this... better on the sales side to go for this little $17 project" (lines 268-269).

**Emotional job:** To feel current and not left behind by technology, resolving a 30-year regret. "never, ever get behind on technology" (line 204); "at least I'm trying. 90% of my generation don't give a  about it" (lines 220-221). Also pure excitement and validation: "I'm literally so excited to talk to Lucas" (line 155); "I'm so  excited" (line 712). He wants to feel like the smart creator/visionary again: "I'm a creator... I can see that 30,000 foot elevation... I just have that vision" (lines 621-627).

**Social job:** To be perceived as the legitimate, experienced authority who dominates and "shuts down" a saturated, sleazy industry. "the funding space is saturated with morons" (line 36); "I'm literally going to shut his doors" (line 118); "I want to literally shut down my competitors" (line 687). He wants the receipts-backed elder-statesman positioning: "I got 58 years... you can't replace experience, period" (lines 911-916); "I have all my receipts... publications and feature magazines" (lines 884-888). He also wants to be seen as a benevolent disruptor who democratizes what others gatekeep: "I'm going to give it to them for $499" what others charge $8K for (lines 566-567).

**Cognitive dissonance / stated vs. underlying driver:**
- Stated reason is commercial ("get that same reoccurring revenue," line 244; the $17 funnel). The underlying driver is more identity and legacy: resolving the Google/AOL regret and proving he can still ride the technology wave at 58. The money is partly secondary; he openly says of his foreclosure business "I ain't doing dick, but I'm helping people like by the hundreds of thousands" (line 242).
- He frames himself as deferential ("you're the expert, you're not me," line 705; "I don't go to a brain surgeon for a toothache," line 707) yet repeatedly drives strategy, dictates offer pricing, and arrives "80, 90% of the way there" by Lucas's own assessment (line 276). His stated humility coexists with strong, fixed opinions, which is a control signal.
- He says the entry product needs a singular clear function (he agrees with Lucas), yet keeps expanding scope: "I might have two or three operating systems with you" (line 33), franchise, retirement, tax, real estate, multiple avatars. Stated focus vs. underlying urge to do everything at once.

## 6. Stall Risk Score

**Score: 2 / 5 (low-to-moderate stall risk; likely to launch reasonably fast).**

Jesse is highly motivated, financially capable, responsive to the point of overnight turnaround, and arrives ~80-90% aligned on positioning, so the will and the raw material to launch are clearly present. The only real drags are an unresolved "credit repair vs. funding" entry-offer decision and a staged, record-breaking data handoff that depends on a sprawling team, both of which could delay a polished v1 if not actively managed.

**Recommended intervention:** Force a single, named Phase-1 entry offer in the next interaction (lead with one of credit-repair diagnostic or funding-plan generator), tie it to one accountable data-handoff owner on Jesse's side, and ship that one $17 function first while staging the larger call corpus in the background.

## Top 3 Takeaways for the PM Team (ranked)

1. **Decide the entry offer; do not let "both" persist.** The one unresolved question in the call is whether the $17 product leads with credit-repair diagnosis or funding-plan generation ("that's where the confusion is because there's value and need in both," line 757). This is the highest-leverage decision and the most likely source of drift. Pick one (or A/B with a single clear primary), give the entry AI one singular function (per Lucas's own principle, lines 716-718), and ship it before expanding to "two or three operating systems."

2. **Treat the data corpus as a staged, record-volume ingestion project with one owner.** Jesse explicitly separates "entry" logins from the real "data" (5,000+ hours of calls plus competitor schools, lines 259-261, 1292-1294) and will "break the record" for platform volume (line 280). Set staged timelines, confirm the pipeline can absorb it, and pin down a single accountable handoff owner on his side to prevent diffusion across his ~50-person Slack and multiple agencies.

3. **Mine his proof and his voice now; they are the marketing and Trust Report.** He hands over exceptional credibility assets ($5M/9mo zero-refund award line 426; five-star testimonials; Lance/Pearl/Jimmy case studies) and quotable, on-brand copy ("free money is free money," "it's not that you're stupid, you're ignorant to it," "formulate your plan first"). Prioritize ingesting testimonials/case studies into the Brain and mirror his exact language plus his underserved-community angle (Spanish/Black markets, lines 1229-1230) in the first marketing preview due Friday.
