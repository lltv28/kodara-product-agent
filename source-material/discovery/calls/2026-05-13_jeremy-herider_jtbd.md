# Jeremy Herider (Harvest) — Onboarding Call JTBD Analysis — 2026-05-13

Client: Jeremy Herider, founder/owner (85%) of Harvest, a done-for-you direct-mail and direct-to-consumer marketing platform for real estate agents.
Call: Kodara onboarding call with Lucas Lee-Tyson, 2026-05-13.
Context: Jeremy already paid $2,500 to start. He previously spoke with "Noor"/"Nora" (Kodara) and watched the 8-minute pre-call video. Harvest plateaus at roughly $50-60k/month and has never crossed 1,000 users.

---

## 1. The Switch Event

**What was happening right before they reached out:** Harvest is stuck at a revenue plateau Jeremy cannot break through on his own. He frames the trigger plainly: "we're just at a plateau. We've hit a spot that on my own, I just can't, I can't seem to break through. We get to that like 50, 60 grand a month." He has done the math on the upside he is missing: "I do not break through. We've never hit 1,000 users... if we can get this business to that just 2,000, 3,000, 4,000 user mark," he projects a "$140 million-dollar business" he could sell "for somewhere between... 70 and 100 million."

**The breaking point / moment of frustration:** 2025 was the specific rupture. After going nationwide in 2023, attrition caught up with the growth cohort all at once. "What happened to us in 25, because we had such a big growth is that what about 65% kept going. So we lost about almost 30, let's just say 40% of the business started falling off, like not renewing... And all of a sudden your numbers go down really quick. And then we're chasing it on the other end... 25, we fell off a cliff." His 12-month campaigns have a 7-month average lifespan: "we have 12 month campaigns and our average stays for about seven months."

**What they tried before and why it fell short:**
- **All-cold, all-manual front end:** "we've never done anything on the front end. Everything has been cold calls, cold emails, referrals, or me doing in-person events." There was never a value-first front end to warm prospects.
- **In-house VA/marketing team + GoHighLevel:** "we use GoHighLevel and we have a ton of really good... follow-ups things and... we're sending them videos and we're doing all the things that you would expect." It runs the nurture but has not fixed retention or the plateau.
- **A "flexible" campaign feature** to make the product less set-and-forget: "We've now opened it up to a little bit more flexible version." But adoption is low: "not enough people know about that. We probably haven't done a good job of promoting it and showing how it's valuable."
- **A capital raise / equity offers** as an alternative path to growth. He turned down a passive-money offer: "I had a guy offer me a million bucks for 20%... I don't want to do anything, but I'll take 20%... I said, I'm going to give up 20% of the company. I want... somebody who can add value, like who can do [things] better than me." Cash alone did not solve the operating problem.

## 3. The Functional, Emotional, and Social Jobs

**Functional job:** Build a value-first front-end funnel (low-ticket AI lead magnet plus AI brain) that warms cold agents, makes Harvest feel "sticky," and lifts retention/user count past the 1,000-user wall toward a saleable 2,000-4,000-user business. "something a little more sticky, something that's going to help them, something that's going to build that confidence in them, something they feel like, man, I can't... live without these guys." Plus offload all marketing/AI build so he stops being the bottleneck: "you guys get it all done, yeah?"

**Emotional job:** To feel he can finally break a ceiling that has personally defeated him, and to stop "chasing it on the other end." He wants the relief of not carrying the build himself ("I was in it... and then I built it") and the confidence that he picked a partner who is real, not another vendor who pawns him off. "I'm in for the fight, man... I don't like to lose."

**Social job:** To be seen as the credible, hands-dirty operator-founder — "the actual guy in the video that showed up on the call" — and ultimately as a winner among his peers. He explicitly wants the status of having Kodara/Lucas in his network: "all of my friends are multi-millionaires... if I go ahead and let this dude Lucas and this happen, they'll be like, what? You got his number?... That's all we all want." He also wants Harvest perceived as built "by agents for agents," and modern despite being analog: "we have to get them to feel like we're doing something that's not [old]."

**Cognitive dissonance / stated reason vs. underlying driver:**
- He frames churn as the agents' fault ("a lot of them, they just don't want to work, man"), but the underlying driver he keeps circling is that *Harvest never proved/visualized its value* ("they're not feeling the value... they don't understand how... what branding actually does"). The product, not just the agent, owns the stickiness gap.
- Stated reason for the engagement is growth/marketing help; the deeper, repeatedly surfaced driver is a **capital raise and an equity partner who adds operating value** ("we're doing a capital raise... I have a ton of equity to... give up if... there's the right [partner]"). He is partly auditing Lucas as a potential strategic/equity partner, not just a vendor — "if you start to think you can be a [partner]."
- He preaches "eat your own dog food" against others, yet his own product's biggest weakness (set-and-forget, no felt value) is the exact passivity he condemns in churned agents — an unacknowledged mirror.

The win condition is "sticky": agents who "feel like... I can't live without these guys." Risk: Jeremy is a fast-moving, three-clicks-and-you-lose-me operator, and his own meeting "on the 18th about some capital investment" suggests parallel timelines that could compete for his attention.

## 5. Product Insights for PM

**Feature gaps or requests implied but not directly stated:**
- **Value-visualization / proof dashboard ("blinking light map").** Strongly implied as the core retention fix. "a lot of it sounds like it's like reporting and just the visualization of it... we got you in front of 12,000 people in these last three months... odds are very high. It's working to some degree." Directly maps to a Trust/Brain-Health-style "your marketing is working" dashboard.
- **Surface and promote the existing "flexible campaign" feature.** "not enough people know about that. We probably haven't done a good job of promoting it and showing how it's valuable." A low-cost stickiness win already in the product.
- **Renewal-moment intervention.** The cancel mechanism is frictionless and triggers at the worst time: "they get the thing to say, hey, time to renew... and you can instantly go, I don't want to renew." PM should design proactive value-proof + save flows ahead of the renewal prompt.
- **Story-driven content generation.** Jeremy's whole marketing thesis is relevancy-via-story over brag-marketing ("sold the house in three days... that's the dumbest marketing"; vs. "if I tell them the story, now I'm way more invested"). The AI brain/content engine should generate story-format collateral, not just "just sold" templates.

**Onboarding friction points (confusion, repeated questions, hesitation):**
- **Tool-stack navigation is a real friction risk.** "ClickUp has 40 [...] channels... I don't even know how to [...] get there, bro." and "Three clicks for me and you lost me." Kodara should pull data themselves (Lucas committed to this) and never make Jeremy hunt through nested docs.
- **He defers on where pieces fit:** re the address-lookup real-estate-AI tool, "I don't know where that fits, but I'll take you as the expert." He wants Kodara to own the architecture decisions, not present him options to sort.

**Trust and confidence signals (relevant to Trust Report / Brain Health dashboard):**
- The single biggest trust signal was founder-shows-up: "You're the first person that's the actual guy in the video that showed up on the call." This is mirror-able in marketing and a trust-anchor in onboarding.
- He validated the AI brain by feel ("does it sound like me?"), exactly Lucas's stated QA bar. Brain Health should foreground "sounds like the founder" confidence early.
- He is explicitly low-ego and pro-feedback: "Even if you tell us it sucks... my identity is not tied to this." Low risk of defensiveness; high openness to PM-led changes.

**Stall risks (anything suggesting he may go quiet or fail to launch):**
- **Attention/competing priorities:** a capital-raise meeting "on the 18th," and he is auditing Lucas as a possible equity partner more than as a vendor. If the partnership/equity conversation stalls, engagement enthusiasm could cool.
- **Three-clicks-and-gone behavior:** any deliverable that requires him to dig will get dropped. Feedback must be one-click and pushed to him directly.
- **He delegates execution to "Moise" / VAs** and rarely operates tools himself, so data-handoff depends on his ops team responding, not just him.
- Lucas flagged the core dependency himself: "the main thing we always ask of you is just timely feedback."

**Messaging / positioning language to mirror in marketing:**
- "We're built by agents for agents... built it for themselves."
- "There's two pillars to marketing, consistency and relevancy" (relevancy = story).
- "We're building equity with the consumer... with the community, with that specific homeowner."
- Analog-as-trust against AI-saturation: "87% would work with a company that showed up in their mailbox more than... in their [...] DMs"; "91% of people would use their realtor again. 12% do."
- "I'm here to protect your commission."
- The dopamine-hit framing for the low-ticket front end (Lucas: "for 17 bucks, this is awesome," which Jeremy affirmed as "the dopamine hit").
