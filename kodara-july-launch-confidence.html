July theme

Launch Confidence

Our clients never use the platform themselves, so the usual SaaS adoption playbook doesn't apply. Our real adoption problem is getting the client to actually launch the AI we built for them. The blocker isn't a missing feature, it's trust: the client has to believe the AI will represent them and their brand well before they'll put it in front of their own audience.

Today we hand them access and ask "what do you think?", which invites nitpicking and doubt. This theme replaces that with evidence: show the client, with data, that their AI is ready and that it's working, so launching stops feeling like a risk.

Note for Alex: everything below is early and rough — directional ideas and quick mockups to react to, not committed scope or final designs. Numbers in the demo are illustrative placeholders.

Why this is the priority (from 15 onboarding calls, May–June 2026)

  • 7/15Fidelity ("will the AI actually be me?") is the single most common anxiety.
  • 13/15Stall on the client-gated content/data handoff before launch — the universal launch blocker.
  • 5/15Hold the "replace me vs. they still want the human" tension.
  • ~0Are AI-skeptical philosophically — the fear is about their expertise surviving the transfer.

Product ideas

rough — for reaction, not commitment
LC-1

AI Readiness Report

New Pre-launch · weeks 1–2

A report we generate once the Brain is trained, then walk through with the client. It tests their AI against a set of benchmarks drawn from their own material and standards and scores coverage area-by-area (voice, method, boundaries, top questions, objections, pricing) with source provenance and gap-flagging. It doubles as the launch gate: launch unlocks once readiness clears a threshold, which turns the client's anxiety into a short, self-serve checklist instead of an open-ended worry.

ProblemClients stall before launch because they don't trust the AI represents them; "what do you think?" makes it worse.
OutcomeTime-to-launch down; % of clients who launch up.
AttacksFidelity anxiety (7/15) and the pre-launch handoff stall (13/15).
Builds onExisting Brain + Interview Mode; needs a benchmark/scoring step.
LC-2

Brain Health Dashboard

New Post-launch · ongoing

After launch, the client still fears "I have no idea what's happening out there," and confidence decays without proof. This is the ongoing view that shows value delivered to them: hours saved, questions their AI answered, satisfaction (with the sample size shown), return rate, when and what their audience asks, and a gap → "teach it now" loop that feeds Interview Mode. Metrics are framed as value and outcomes, never as time-in-app.

ProblemPost-launch confidence fades; clients can't see the AI working, so doubt and churn creep back.
OutcomeRetention/renewal up (they can see it working); the Brain keeps improving from real gaps.
Attacks"Lost control / don't know what's happening" doubt; the renewal-window risk.
Builds onUsage + satisfaction instrumentation (dependency, see open questions).
LC-3

Interview Mode 2.0

Upgrade Engine behind LC-1 & LC-2

Interview Mode already exists but is essentially unused, and today it only works reactively after lots of live usage. We upgrade it into the capture engine behind both screens: structured topic coverage, proactive gap detection (pre-launch from the benchmarks, post-launch from real unanswered questions), and quick voice-answer capture ("just talk, it trains itself"). It produces the Readiness score and resolves the Brain Health gaps. It's also the mechanic for the required ~20–30 min "AI Brain Interview" before launch, so the client feels involved and is personalizing the AI, without being handed a raw chat to get in their own head about.

ProblemThe existing feature is unused and only reacts after launch; clients have no guided way to improve the Brain.
OutcomeRaises fidelity, fills gaps fast, and channels client involvement productively.
Shown asThe left rail of the Brain Health screen and the gap-filling flow in the Readiness Report.
StatusUpgrade of an existing feature, not greenfield.
